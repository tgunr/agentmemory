### 🤖 Assistant — 2026-08-14T21:09:35Z

**The most relevant free STL files are for a DIY “Diaphragm Piston” (also called rolling piston or top-hat piston) by ThinkingandTinkering / Robert Murray-Smith.**

This uses a 3D-printed cylinder + piston with a latex glove finger as the rolling diaphragm seal. It provides very low friction/hysteresis and works well at low pressures (demonstrated by simply blowing into the ports).

### Main files
- **Diaphragm Piston body/parts**: [Thingiverse thing:6819005](https://www.thingiverse.com/thing:6819005)

**Materials needed** (beyond printed parts):

- Graphite (dry lubricant)
- Optional: cyanoacrylate glue for assembly

### Other related context
A 2019 academic paper describes low-cost SLA 3D-printed rolling-diaphragm cylinders (using commercial Dia·Com D-137-137 diaphragms) for accurate force control / robotic transmissions. The paper itself is available on Zenodo, but it does **not** appear to include public STL files of the hardware; it focuses on embedding commercial diaphragms into printed cylinders.<grok:render card_id="f97a42" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">0</argument></grok:render>

Commercial rolling diaphragms (fabric-reinforced elastomer “top-hat” style) are sold by companies such as Dia·Com, Bellofram, etc., for pneumatic/hydraulic actuators. The DIY latex-glove version above is a simple, printable approximation popular in the maker community.

Search Thingiverse, Printables, or MakerWorld for “diaphragm piston” or “rolling piston” if you need variants. Let me know if you want links to similar mechanisms (e.g., other diaphragm pumps/engines) or help with print settings!

### 🤖 Assistant — 2026-08-14T21:20:42Z

**Small, low-cost rolling-diaphragm cylinders** are available in both commercial mini versions and very inexpensive DIY/3D-printed designs. These use a flexible “top-hat” elastomer (often fabric-reinforced) that rolls between the piston and cylinder wall, delivering near-zero friction, no stiction, no blow-by leakage, and excellent sensitivity/repeatability without lubrication.

### Lowest-Cost Options (DIY / Maker-Friendly)
These are the most affordable for prototypes or small projects:

- **Latex glove + 3D-printed parts** (extreme low cost — materials under $5 + print time):  
  A natural latex glove finger acts as the rolling diaphragm. Free STL files exist for a simple single-acting piston/cylinder (Thingiverse thing:6819005) and a related air engine (thing:6822399). Graphite powder provides dry lubrication. Works well at low pressures; demonstrated by simply blowing into the ports.
