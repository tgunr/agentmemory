### 👤 User

What is a good dimension for a motivation poster 12" wide

---

### 🤖 Assistant

For a motivational poster that is 12 inches wide, the most common and aesthetically pleasing dimensions follow standard aspect ratios used in printing and framing.

A very popular choice is the **12" x 18"** dimension, which uses a 2:3 aspect ratio. This is a standard small poster size that feels elongated enough to provide plenty of room for a bold headline and a central image without feeling cramped.

Another excellent option is **12" x 16"**, which follows a 3:4 aspect ratio. This size is slightly more compact and works well if your design is more focused on a single, powerful quote or a square-ish graphic.

If you are looking for a perfectly balanced, classic look, you might consider **12" x 12"**. Square posters have become increasingly popular for modern, minimalist motivational designs, especially those intended for smaller office spaces or gallery walls.

For a more dramatic, vertical impact, you could even go as long as **12" x 24"**. This "slim" format is great for tall typography or designs that

lead the eye upward, though it may require custom framing.

### 👤 User

Tried an engrave using quick engrave using outline mode on the smallest text with 15º V bit at 0.1" depth and some of the letters like e and o are blowing out on a white oak board. Recommendations? Larger poster size to permit larger text?

---

### 🤖 Assistant

White oak can be tricky for *small, detailed text*, especially with a **15º V‑bit** at **0.1" depth**—that combination can easily blow out enclosed letters (o, e, a, etc.) because:

- 15° bits are extremely steep → they cut very deep with very small movements.
- At 0.1" depth, the cut becomes wide quickly.
- Oak has open grain that splinters around tight corners.

Here are practical recommendations before resorting to making the whole poster larger:

---

### **1. Reduce Your Max Depth**
For tiny text, **0.03"–0.05" deep** is often enough.  
This reduces blow‑out dramatically because the V‑bit doesn’t widen the stroke as much.

---

### **2. Switch to a 30º or 60º V-bit**
If possible:

- **30º bit** → best for extremely small text.
- **60º bit** → more forgiving, less fragile than 15º.

A 15º bit is great for *micro-detail* but cuts too aggressively for hardwood unless depth is very shallow.

---

### **3. Slow Down and Use a Finishing Pass**
Try:

- **Slower feed rate** (e.g., 40–60 IPM)
- **Shallower first

pass** (0.01"–0.02")
- Then a **cleanup pass** at full depth

Hardwoods respond better to light, controlled cuts.

---

### **4. Use "Flat Depth" for V-Carving if Your Software Allows It**
Set a **flat depth limit** of **0.04"–0.06"** so the tool never dives too deep.

---

### **5. Seal the Wood Before Engraving**
Brush on:

- shellac, or
- lacquer sanding sealer

This stiffens the wood fibers so small text cuts cleaner.

---

### **6. Increase Text Size Only as a Last Resort**
If you're doing extremely tiny text (less than ~0.20" tall), increasing size may be necessary for oak.  
Hardwoods with open grain don’t handle micro‑engraving as well as maple or MDF.

But you likely don’t need to upscale the entire poster. Often just increasing the smallest text or switching bits fixes the issue.

---

### If you want sizing guidance:
For crisp V‑carving in oak:

- Minimum letter height: **≥ 0.25"** (with a 60º bit)
- Minimum letter height for 15º bit: **≥ 0.20"**, **shallow depth only**
