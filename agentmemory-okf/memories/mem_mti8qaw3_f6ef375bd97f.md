### 🤖 Assistant

Melatonin is a naturally occurring hormone produced by the pineal gland in your brain. It plays a critical role in regulating your body's circadian rhythm, which acts as an internal 24-hour clock that tells your body when it is time to sleep and wake up. Production typically increases in response to darkness and decreases when it is light.

As of 2026, it remains one of the most popular over-the-counter sleep aids in the United States. Below is a summary of its uses, safety profile, and recommended dosing.

### Common Uses and Benefits
Melatonin is generally most effective for sleep issues related to timing (circadian rhythm) rather than general insomnia.
*   **Jet Lag:** Helps the body adjust more quickly when traveling across multiple time zones [NCCIH](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/melatonin-what-you-need-to-know).
*   **Delayed Sleep Phase:** Helps individuals who naturally stay up very late and have trouble waking up in the morning fall asleep earlier [Mayo

Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/drugs-supplements-melatonin/art-20363071).
*   **Shift Work:** May provide some assistance for those working non-traditional hours, though research is less conclusive for this group [Drugs.com](https://www.drugs.com/melatonin.html).
*   **Pre-Surgery Anxiety:** Evidence suggests it can reduce anxiety levels before and after surgical procedures [NCCIH](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/melatonin-what-you-need-to-know).

### Recommended Dosage
In the United States, melatonin is classified as a dietary supplement and is not FDA-approved to treat medical conditions. Experts generally recommend the "start low" approach:
*   **Starting Dose:** 0.5 mg to 1 mg taken 30 to 60 minutes before bedtime.
*   **Typical Range:** 1 mg to 3 mg is sufficient for most adults.
*   **Efficiency:** Doses higher than 5 mg are rarely more effective and significantly increase the risk of side effects like vivid dreams or a "melatonin hangover" the next morning [Drugs.com](https://www.drugs.com

/melatonin.html).

### Side Effects and Risks
While generally safe for short-term use, it can cause the following:
*   **Common:** Headache, dizziness, nausea, and daytime drowsiness [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/healthy-lifestyle/adult-health/expert-answers/melatonin-side-effects/faq-20057874).
*   **Less Common:** Vivid dreams or nightmares, irritability, and mild anxiety [WebMD](https://www.webmd.com/vitamins/ai/ingredientmono-940/melatonin).
*   **Contamination and Labeling:** Recent studies have found that some over-the-counter melatonin products—especially gummies—contain significantly different amounts of the hormone than what is listed on the label, sometimes up to 347% of the stated dose [NCCIH](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/melatonin-what-you-need-to-know).

### Important Precautions
*   **Interactions:** Melatonin can interact with blood thinners, diabetes medications, seizure medications, and blood pressure drugs.
*   **Children:** Melatonin should only be given to children under

the supervision of a pediatrician. There are concerns about its effect on hormonal development and puberty with long-term use [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/healthy-lifestyle/adult-health/expert-answers/melatonin-side-effects/faq-20057874).
*   **Autoimmune Disease:** People with autoimmune conditions are often advised to avoid melatonin as it may stimulate the immune system.

If you have persistent sleep issues, you may want to discuss **Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Insomnia (CBT-I)** with a healthcare provider, as it is often considered the gold standard for long-term insomnia treatment [NCCIH](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/melatonin-what-you-need-to-know).
