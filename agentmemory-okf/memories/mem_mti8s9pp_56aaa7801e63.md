### 🤖 Assistant

Based on the prominent, open grain pattern and the light, creamy coloration shown in the image, this piece of wood is very likely **White Oak** or **Ash**.

The distinctive cathedral grain (the arch-like patterns in the center) and the strong vertical lines are characteristic of species in the oak family. In particular, it looks like a flat-sawn board of White Oak, which is often favored for its durability and resistance to moisture. Ash is another strong possibility because it shares a very similar grain structure, though it typically lacks the "ray fleck" patterns sometimes seen in oak and often has a slightly more uniform, bright white-to-light-brown tone.

Since you are located in Texas, White Oak is extremely common in local lumber yards and construction. If you want to be certain, you can check the end grain; oak will have visible pores that are quite distinct, whereas ash pores are arranged slightly differently. Another indicator is the smell when sanded: oak has a distinct, somewhat acidic or

"tannic" scent, while ash is much more neutral.
