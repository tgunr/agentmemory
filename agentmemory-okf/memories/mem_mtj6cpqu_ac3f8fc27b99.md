## Indiana Law on Evicting Renters with Verbal Agreements

**Verbal rental agreements are legally valid in Indiana.** A landlord-tenant relationship can be created without a written lease agreement, and this type of tenancy is called a general tenancy or at-will tenancy, which are usually treated as month-to-month tenancies.

### Notice Requirements

For tenants with only verbal agreements:

**30-Day Notice Required:** In Indiana, a landlord can evict a tenant who does not have a lease ("tenant at will") by giving the tenant proper 30-days' notice to move out. A tenant on a month-to-month lease agreement needs a 30-day notice.

### The Eviction Process

Even with a verbal agreement, landlords must follow proper legal procedures:

1. **Proper Notice:** The landlord must give proper notice under Indiana law prior to filing an eviction action so the tenant has an opportunity to move or to create a new agreement with the landlord

2. **Court Filing Required:** Under Indiana law, landlords must file a lawsuit to evict a tenant from their property. Once they receive a judgment from the court, a law enforcement officer will remove the tenant from the property if necessary
