How to train a chicken killing dog with a mini educator

---

### 🤖 Assistant

Training a dog to stop killing chickens using a Mini Educator (e-collar) is a high-stakes process that requires precision, timing, and a solid foundation of basic obedience. Because predatory behavior is self-rewarding, the goal is to create a clear "boundary" where the dog understands that chasing or harming livestock is strictly off-limits.

### Phase 1: Foundation and Calibration
Before introducing the dog to chickens, you must ensure the collar is fitted correctly and the dog understands what the stimulation means.

*   **Proper Fit:** The contact points must touch the skin. For thick-coated or muscular breeds, you may need longer contact points or to place the collar higher on the neck, just behind the ears [JustAnswer](https://www.justanswer.com/pet-dog/kg486-recently-converted-mini-educator-ecollar-training.html).
*   **Find the Working Level:** Start at level 0 and slowly increase the dial while the dog is calm. The "working level" is the lowest number where you see a physical sign the dog feels it,

such as an ear twitch or a head tilt. This is usually between levels 3 and 12 [The Naked Dog](https://www.thenakeddogtraining.com/traininghowtos/2020/2/18/thinking-of-getting-an-e-collar-how-to-start-out-the-right-way).
*   **Conditioning:** Teach the dog that the stimulation is a "leash tug" from a distance. Practice known commands like "Sit" or "Come" in low-distraction areas, using the stimulation to reinforce the command until the dog responds reliably [Reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/OpenDogTraining/comments/1ag51fw/in_depth_steps-to-correctly-use-ecollar/).

### Phase 2: Controlled Exposure
Never start this training with the dog off-leash or with loose chickens.

*   **The Setup:** Place the chickens in a secure coop or run. Keep your dog on a long training lead (15–30 feet) while wearing the Mini Educator.
*   **Neutrality Training:** Walk the dog past the chickens at a distance where they are aware of the birds but not yet lunging. Reward the dog for looking at you instead of the chickens.
*   **The

"Leave It" Command:** If the dog fixates on a chicken, give a firm "Leave It" command. If they do not immediately break eye contact, use a momentary stimulation at their working level. The moment they look away, stop the stimulation and reward them heavily with high-value treats like hot dogs or real chicken [JustAnswer](https://www.justanswer.com/pet-dog/kg486-recently-converted-mini-educator-ecollar-training.html).

### Phase 3: Correcting the Predatory Drive
Once the dog understands "Leave It" around the coop, you must address the actual "strike" or chase behavior.

*   **Increasing Intensity:** Predatory drive often overrides low-level stimulation. If the dog ignores the "Leave It" and attempts to lung or chase, you will likely need to "up the ante" by increasing the level significantly (often 20–30 points higher than the base level) to break the focus [The Naked Dog](https://www.thenakeddogtraining.com/traininghowtos/2020/2/18/thinking-of-getting-an-e-collar-how-to-start-out-the-right-way).
*   **Timing

is Critical:** The correction must happen the *instant* the dog decides to load (stiffen, stare, or crouch) or chase. Correcting after the dog has already caught a chicken is often too late to be effective for learning.
*   **The "Stop" Signal:** Use the "S" (Stimulation) button to provide a continuous correction until the dog stops the unwanted behavior. As soon as the dog stops or turns back to you, release the button immediately.
