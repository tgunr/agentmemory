### 👤 User

What solvent to use to remove dried latex paint from sprayer?

---

### 🤖 Assistant

To remove dried latex paint from a sprayer, you can use several common solvents and household items. Since latex paint is water-based, the goal is to use a substance that can break down the acrylic resins that have hardened.

### Recommended Solvents
*   **Denatured Alcohol or Rubbing Alcohol (Isopropyl):** This is one of the most effective solvents for dissolving dried latex and acrylic paint. It breaks the bond between the paint particles, allowing them to liquefy so they can be wiped or flushed out [DIY Geeks](https://diygeeks.com/things-to-dissolve-latex-paint/).
*   **Ammonia:** A strong solution of hot water and ammonia is a classic professional remedy for loosening dried latex. It is particularly effective for soaking small metal parts like tips and filters [The Garage Journal](https://www.garagejournal.com/forum/threads/how-to-clean-latex-paint-from-spray-gun.518587/).
*   **Acetone:** A very strong solvent that will quickly dissolve latex paint. However, use it with caution as it can damage plastic

or rubber seals and gaskets inside some sprayers if left to soak for too long [DIY Geeks](https://diygeeks.com/things-to-dissolve-latex-paint/).
*   **Commercial Paint Removers:** Products like **Goof Off** (which often contains Xylene) or **Goo Gone Latex Paint Clean-Up** are specifically formulated to tackle dried latex spills and tool cleaning [Brad the Painter](https://www.bradthepainter.com/latex-paint-remover/), [Goo Gone](https://googone.com/latex-paint-clean-up-spray).

### Cleaning Steps
1.  **Disassemble the Sprayer:** Take apart the gun, removing the tip, guard, and filters. These are the areas where paint most commonly clogs.
2.  **Soak the Parts:** Place the metal components in a container of denatured alcohol or a warm water and ammonia solution. Let them soak for 30–60 minutes.
3.  **Scrub Gently:** Use a soft-bristle brush (like a toothbrush) or specialized sprayer cleaning brushes to scrub away the softened paint. Avoid using metal scrapers that could scratch the precision surfaces of the

tip [Brad the Painter](https://www.bradthepainter.com/latex-paint-remover/).
4.  **Flush the System:** If the internal lines are clogged, you may need to run the solvent through the sprayer. After using a chemical solvent, always follow up by flushing the system thoroughly with warm, soapy water.
5.  **Lubricate:** Once clean and dry, apply a throat seal lubricant or a light pump armor/preservative to prevent internal parts from sticking or rusting before the next use [The Garage Journal](https://www.garagejournal.com/forum/threads/how-to-clean-latex-paint-from-spray-gun.518587/).

**Safety Note:** When using strong solvents like acetone or xylene, work in a well-ventilated area and wear chemical-resistant gloves and eye protection.

### Thinning oil-based paint for an air sprayer (HVLP/LVLP/conventional)

#### 1) Use the right thinner
- **Alkyd / oil-based enamel (most “oil-based” house paints):** use **mineral spirits / paint thinner**.
- **Oil-based polyurethane / varnish:** often sprays best with **mineral spirits** (some brands allow **naphtha** for faster flash—check the can).
- **True lacquer:** use **lacquer thinner** (not mineral spirits).

If you’re not sure, the can will usually say **“thin/clean up with mineral spirits”** or similar—follow that.

#### 2) How much to thin (typical starting points)
These vary by gun, tip size, and product, but good starting ranges:

- **HVLP/LVLP:** **10–20%** mineral spirits by volume  
- **Conventional siphon/gravity:** **5–15%**  
- **Heavier enamels/primers:** may need **15–25%** (but avoid over-thinning)
