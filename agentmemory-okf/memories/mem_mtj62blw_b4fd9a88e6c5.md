### 🤖 Assistant — 2025-06-01T11:51:19Z

Newborn goats (kids) typically need several vaccinations to protect against common diseases. Here's the standard vaccination schedule:

**Essential vaccines:**

**CDT vaccine** (Clostridium perfringens types C & D, plus tetanus) - This is the most important core vaccine. Give the first dose at 6-8 weeks of age, with a booster 3-4 weeks later. Annual boosters are then needed.

**Additional vaccines to consider:**

**Pneumonia vaccines** - Often recommended in areas where respiratory diseases are common. These may include vaccines for Mannheimia haemolytica and Pasteurella multocida.

**Caseous lymphadenitis (CL)** - In areas where this bacterial infection is prevalent, vaccination may be recommended starting around 6 months of age.

**Important timing considerations:**

- Kids receive initial immunity from colostrum (first milk) if the mother was properly vaccinated
- Don't vaccinate too early (before 6 weeks) as maternal antibodies can interfere with vaccine effectiveness
- Pregnant does should be vaccinated 4-6 weeks before kidding to ensure protective antibodies in colostrum

**Regional variations:**

Some areas may have additional vaccine recommendations based on local disease risks, such as rabies in certain regions or other region-specific concerns.

It's crucial to work with a veterinarian familiar with goat health and local disease patterns to develop an appropriate vaccination schedule for your specific situation and location. They can advise on timing, additional vaccines that might be needed, and proper vaccine storage and administration techniques.
