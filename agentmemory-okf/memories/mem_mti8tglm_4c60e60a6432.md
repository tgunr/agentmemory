### 🤖 Assistant

Yes, the message I just drafted is a **textbook example of a phishing scam**.

While the text itself mimics the language Apple uses for legitimate storage warnings, there are several "red flags" in that draft (and in similar real-world messages) that indicate it is a scam:

#### Red Flags to Look For:

*   **The Sender's Address:** Scammers often use addresses that look official at a glance but are fake, such as `no-reply@storage-verification-icloud.com`. A real email from Apple will always come from an `@apple.com` or `@icloud.com` domain.
*   **Sense of Urgency and Threats:** Phrases like "immediately," "avoid permanent data loss," or "scheduled for deactivation" are designed to make you panic so you click the link without thinking.
*   **Suspicious Links:** In the draft, I used a shortened link (`bit.ly`). Real Apple emails will direct you to `apple.com` or ask you to go directly to the **Settings** app on your iPhone or Mac.
*   **Generic Greeting:** While this specific draft used your email address,

many scams start with "Dear Customer" or "Dear User" because they don't actually know your name.

#### How to Verify Safely:

If you are worried that your iCloud storage actually is full, **never click a link in an email.** Instead, check the status directly on your device:

1.  **On iPhone/iPad:** Go to **Settings** > **[Your Name]** > **iCloud**.
2.  **On a Mac:** Go to **System Settings** > **Apple ID** > **iCloud**.
3.  **Web:** Log in directly at [icloud.com](https://www.icloud.com).

If your storage is truly full, you will see the notification there. If it isn't, you can safely delete the email and report it as junk.
