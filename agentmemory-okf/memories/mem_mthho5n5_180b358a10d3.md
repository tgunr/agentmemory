### 🤖 Assistant — 2025-02-25T23:07:44Z

There haven’t been any widely reported *major* outbreaks of highly pathogenic avian influenza (HPAI), commonly known as bird flu, specifically isolated to eastern Texas in recent years—at least not on the scale of some other regions like the Texas Panhandle or states like California and Ohio. Eastern Texas isn’t typically highlighted as a distinct epicenter in the data compared to other parts of the state or country. However, bird flu has absolutely made its mark across Texas as a whole, and eastern counties haven’t been entirely spared.

Since the nationwide HPAI outbreak kicked off in 2022, Texas has seen its share of detections in poultry, wild birds, and even dairy cattle. The Texas Animal Health Commission has logged cases across various counties, with some notable hits closer to eastern Texas. For instance, in November 2023, HPAI was confirmed in Ellis County—just southeast of Dallas, which could be considered on the western edge of what people might call eastern Texas. Earlier, in September 2022, Dallas County itself reported a detection. Further south, Brazoria County, near the Gulf Coast, had a case confirmed in January 2025. These spots suggest the virus has popped up in areas that could loosely fall under an eastern Texas umbrella, depending on how you draw the line.

The big story in Texas, though, has been more tied to the Panhandle and northern regions. In March 2024, HPAI H5N1 was detected in dairy cattle in the Panhandle, marking a first for the U.S. and sparking a multi-state outbreak. This tied into a human case in April 2024, where a dairy worker in Texas tested positive after cow exposure—again, linked to the Panhandle, not the east. Poultry outbreaks have also hit hard statewide, with a major culling of 1.6 million laying hens in Gonzales County (central-southeast Texas) in April 2024 after a positive test. That’s not quite eastern Texas either, but it shows how the virus has radiated beyond just one region.

Wild birds, the main spreaders, have carried HPAI across Texas too. Counties like Harris (home to Houston, firmly in eastern Texas) and Wharton have seen detections in wild populations, with posts on X noting cases in places like Galveston and Travis counties as recently as January 2025. These aren’t “major outbreaks” in the sense of massive poultry losses or human health crises, but they confirm the virus is circulating.

So, eastern Texas? No single, headline-grabbing catastrophe stands out as "major" just there. But the region’s not immune—scattered cases in poultry and wild birds show it’s part of the broader Texas picture. The real heavy hitters have been elsewhere in the state, especially where dairy and big poultry operations cluster. Still, with wild birds migrating and the virus adapting, it’s worth keeping an eye on the whole state, east included.
