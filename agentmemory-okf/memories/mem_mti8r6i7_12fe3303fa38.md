### 🤖 Assistant

Based on the grain pattern in the first image and the end grain visible in the second, this appears to be **White Oak** plywood or veneer.

The first image shows the characteristic cathedral grain and open pores common in the oak family. The second image, showing the edge profile, reveals very distinct, large open pores and a biological structure consistent with oak. The tan-to-wheat color is specifically indicative of white oak rather than red oak, which typically has a more pronounced pinkish or reddish undertone.

It's also worth noting that the second image shows several "wormholes" or small boring exit holes. This is common in certain grades of oak (often called "wormy oak") or can sometimes occur in lumber that was sitting in a mill or warehouse. If this is new material you just purchased, you may want to check if the holes were a stylistic choice (like "rustic" grade) or if there are signs of active boring, though in kiln-dried plywood, these are almost always old and inactive.
