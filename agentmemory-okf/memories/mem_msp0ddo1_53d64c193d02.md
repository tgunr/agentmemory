Great question — this is the "we are stardust" puzzle, and the resolution is that the Sun is a **third-generation star**, not a first-generation one.

## The timeline that makes it work

- **Universe forms** ~13.8 billion years ago. Big Bang nucleosynthesis makes only the three lightest elements: **hydrogen, helium, and a trace of lithium.** No carbon, iron, gold, oxygen, etc. — those require stellar furnaces.
- **First stars (Population III)** ignite a few hundred million years later, built from pristine H/He. They were huge, hot, and short-lived (a few million years). They fused H→He, then He→C/O, and in their death throes (supernovae) forged everything up to iron by fusion and everything *heavier* than iron by rapid neutron capture (the r-process).
- **~9 billion years pass** — that's the key number. That's enormous slack for *many* generations of stars to be born, cook elements, and die, seeding the interstellar medium with "metals" (astronomers' word for anything heavier than helium).
- **~4.6 billion years ago**, a cold molecular cloud — already enriched with heavy elements from those earlier dead stars — collapsed under gravity. That collapse *is* the Sun and the protoplanetary disk that became the planets.
