### 👤 User

How many species on earth have spontaneous sex?

---

### 🤖 Assistant

In biological terms, the concept of "spontaneous sex" is most accurately interpreted as **spontaneous parthenogenesis** (often called "virgin births"), where a female of a species that typically reproduces sexually suddenly produces offspring without a male.

While the vast majority of the millions of species on Earth reproduce sexually, scientists have documented **hundreds of species** capable of this spontaneous shift.

### Species Known for Spontaneous Parthenogenesis
This phenomenon has been documented across a wide variety of the tree of life, including:
*   **Reptiles:** The **Asian Water Dragon** and the **Komodo Dragon** are famous examples. In 2016, a female water dragon at the National Zoo produced a daughter despite never being with a male.
*   **Sharks:** Cases have been documented in **Blacktip sharks** and **Zebra sharks** kept in aquarium settings.
*   **Snakes:** The **Checkered Gartersnake** and various species of boas and pythons have shown this ability.
*   **Birds:** It has been observed

in **California Condors**, turkeys, and chickens.
*   **Insects and Crustaceans:** Many species of wasps, aphids, and bees utilize this as a standard or emergency reproductive strategy.

### The "Multiple Sexes" Phenomenon
If you are referring to species that have more than the standard two biological sexes (male and female), there are several unique cases:
*   **Fungi:** Some species, like *Schizophyllum commune*, have over **23,000 mating types** (the fungal equivalent of sexes).
*   **Protozoans:** The single-celled *Tetrahymena thermophila* has **seven distinct sexes**. Any individual can mate with an individual of any sex except its own.
*   **Social Insects:** Some species of **ants and bees** are argued to have three or four biological "sexes" because their social system requires three parental types (e.g., a queen, a drone, and a specific worker caste) to maintain the population's genetic structure.

### Why is this rare?
Most multicellular life maintains two sexes because it is the most efficient

way to ensure genetic diversity while minimizing the "search cost" of finding a partner. Requiring three or more individuals to meet simultaneously for a single reproductive event is mathematically much harder and less stable over evolutionary time.

For more detailed information on the biological mechanics of these species, you can refer to resources like [CNN Science](https://www.cnn.com/2021/12/27/world/virgin-births-parthenogenesis-partner-scn) or the [University of Arizona Research](https://news.arizona.edu/news/new-research-helps-explain-diversity-life-and-paradox-sex).

No, I mean how many species can engage in sexual coupling at will rather than being in heat?

---

### 🤖 Assistant

There isn’t a known, credible “number of species” for that, mainly because **“at-will sex” isn’t a standard biological category that’s been systematically tallied across all species**.

What biologists *do* use is whether a species shows **oestrus/estrus (“in heat”)**—a period when females are most receptive—versus having sexual behavior that happens **outside oestrus** (sometimes for social bonding, conflict reduction, etc.). Importantly, even in species that copulate outside fertile periods, behavior is still influenced by hormones, seasonality, social context, etc., so it’s rarely “at will” in the human sense.
