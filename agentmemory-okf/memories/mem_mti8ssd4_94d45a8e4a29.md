### Lowest-cost cleaning/thinning fluid for oil-based paints + solvent cleaning

If your goal is **cheapest per usable gallon** for **thinning oil-based paint** and **cleaning brushes/tools**, the lowest-cost option is almost always:

#### 1) **Mineral spirits (a.k.a. paint thinner / white spirits)** — usually the cheapest overall “do-everything” choice
- **Use cases:** thinning most oil-based paints, cleaning brushes/rollers/spray equipment, general degreasing.
- **Why it’s usually the lowest cost:** widely available and often sold in **1-gallon and 5-gallon** containers at commodity pricing.
- **What to look for on the label:** “**Mineral Spirits**”, “**Odorless Mineral Spirits (OMS)**” (OMS is often a bit pricier), or “**Paint Thinner**” (sometimes a blend—still typically cheapest).

#### 2) **Kerosene** — sometimes cheaper, but more tradeoffs (and not always ideal for paint thinning)
- **Use cases:** parts washing, some degreasing/cleaning.
- **Tradeoffs:** can leave an oily residue; odor; **not always

recommended** as a thinner for many modern oil-based coatings (check your paint can/TDS).

#### 3) **Acetone / MEK / lacquer thinner** — often *not* the lowest cost and can be the wrong solvent
- **Use cases:** fast, aggressive cleaning (especially for adhesives/resins), but **often not appropriate** for thinning oil-based paints unless the coating specifically allows it.
- **Downside:** more volatile, harsher, can damage plastics/finishes, and can flash too quickly.
