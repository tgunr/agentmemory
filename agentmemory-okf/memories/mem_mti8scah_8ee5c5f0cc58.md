### Making a Thin Shellac Sealer for MDF

MDF is very porous, especially on the edges, so a **thin “wash coat” of shellac** works well to seal it before paint or topcoat.

#### 1. Choose the right shellac

- Use **dewaxed shellac** if you plan to:
  - Paint over it (especially with water-based paints), or  
  - Apply polyurethane or other clear finishes on top.  
- You can buy:
  - **Premixed shellac** (e.g., Zinsser SealCoat – already dewaxed, ~2 lb cut), or  
  - **Shellac flakes + denatured alcohol** to mix your own.

#### 2. Target cut for sealing MDF

For sealing MDF, aim for a **1 lb cut or thinner**:

- **If using premixed 2 lb cut shellac (like SealCoat):**
  - Mix **1 part shellac : 1 part denatured alcohol**  
  - This gives you roughly a **1 lb cut**, great as a sealer.

- **If mixing from flakes**:
  - A rough rule:  
    - **1 lb cut** ≈ 1 oz shellac flakes (by weight) in 8 fl oz alcohol  
    - For a very thin first pass, you can go even weaker, e.g. **½ lb cut**:  
      - ½ oz flakes in 8 fl

oz alcohol.

Let it dissolve fully (often overnight), stirring occasionally.

#### 3. Surface prep

1. Sand MDF faces and edges with **180–220 grit**.
2. Remove dust with a **vacuum and tack cloth** or a lightly damp lint-free rag.
3. Pay special attention to **edges** and routed profiles; they’re the thirstiest.

#### 4. Applying the thin shellac

1. Use a **good quality bristle brush**, foam brush, or small roller.  
2. Flood on the thinned shellac, especially on **edges**, and then:
   - Quickly **brush out** to avoid runs and lap marks.
3. It should **soak in quickly** and look almost like you just dampened the surface.

#### 5. Drying and sanding

- Shellac dries fast:
  - Usually **15–30 minutes** per coat depending on temperature/humidity.
- Once dry, lightly sand with **220–320 grit**:
  - You’re just knocking down raised fibers; don’t sand through completely.
- Wipe off dust.

#### 6. Additional coats (optional but helpful on MDF)

- For edges and highly absorbent areas:
  - Apply **2–3 thin coats**

of the same thinned shellac,
  - Sand very lightly between coats.
- After this, the MDF should feel **smooth and less “fuzzy”**, and it won’t drink in paint or finish as badly.

#### 7. Topcoating

Once sealed and sanded smooth, you can:

- **Prime and paint** (latex or enamel), or  
- Apply clear finishes (polyurethane, lacquer, waterborne, etc. – dewaxed shellac only).

For a CNC‑carved photo in MDF where you’ve:

- Painted it black
- Sanded back the surface so the **carved lines stay dark** and the flat areas are clean

you basically have an *inlay-style* piece. The best finish depends on the look and durability you want and whether it’s indoors or outdoors.

### 1. Most common choice: Clear satin or matte topcoat

If this is **indoor decor** (sign, photo plaque, wall art), the usual approach:

1. **Seal first (optional but recommended for MDF):**  
   - A thin coat of **dewaxed shellac** (like [Zinsser SealCoat](https://www.rustoleum.com/product-catalog/consumer-brands/zinsser/sealcoat) diluted 1:1 with denatured alcohol) over everything.  
   - Lightly sand with 320 grit after it dries.  
   - This locks MDF fibers and the black pigment in place.
