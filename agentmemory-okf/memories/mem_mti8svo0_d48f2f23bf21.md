### 👤 User

is isopropyl alcohol good for UV ink cleaning?

---

### 🤖 Assistant

Isopropyl alcohol (IPA) is a common and effective solvent for cleaning UV ink, but its suitability depends heavily on whether the ink is **liquid (uncured)** or **solid (cured)**, and what material you are cleaning.

### 1. Cleaning Uncured (Liquid) UV Ink
IPA is excellent for cleaning up wet UV ink spills or wiping down printer parts (like wiper blades or capping stations).
*   **Effectiveness:** High. It dissolves the liquid monomers and photoinitiators quickly.
*   **Recommendation:** Use **99% Isopropyl Alcohol** for the best results, as lower concentrations (like 70% rubbing alcohol) contain more water, which can cause streaking or interfere with the ink's chemistry [PrintPlanet](https://printplanet.com/threads/help-cleaning-substrates-with-alcohol-or-acetone.21203/).

### 2. Cleaning Cured (Hardened) UV Ink
Once UV ink has been "cured" by UV light, it becomes a cross-linked polymer (essentially a plastic layer), making it much harder to remove.
*   **Effectiveness:** Moderate to Low. IPA will not

instantly dissolve cured ink, but a prolonged soak can sometimes "curl" or swell the ink, making it easier to scrape off [Signs101](https://www.signs101.com/threads/cleaning-uv-ink-off-acrylic.135641/).
*   **Method:** Saturate a paper towel with 99% IPA, place it over the cured ink, and let it sit for 5–10 minutes. You can then use a plastic razor blade to gently lift the ink [Signs101](https://www.signs101.com/threads/cleaning-uv-ink-off-acrylic.135641/).

### 3. Risks and Material Compatibility
The biggest danger with using IPA for UV ink cleaning is the damage it can do to the underlying surface:
*   **Acrylic (Plexiglass):** IPA can cause "crazing" (tiny internal cracks) in acrylic, especially on laser-cut or flame-polished edges. If you must use it on acrylic, avoid the edges and do not let it soak for more than a few minutes [Signs101](https://www.signs101.com/threads/cleaning-uv-ink-off-acrylic.135641/).
*   **Polycarbonate:** IPA is generally safer on polycarbonate than acrylic, but it can still

cause surface dulling over time.
*   **3D Resin Prints:** IPA is the standard cleaning agent for removing uncured resin from 3D prints, though some users prefer specialized cleaners or ultrasonic baths with water to avoid the "slimy" residue IPA can sometimes leave [Reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/resinprinting/comments/g4egsm/will_rubbing_alcohol_work_for_cleaning_prints/).

**Pro Tip:** If IPA isn't strong enough for cured ink, some professionals use **denatured alcohol** or specialized **UV wash solutions** provided by the ink

manufacturer, which are designed to break down the polymer bonds without damaging the printer hardware [Signs101](https://www.signs101.com/threads/cleaning-uv-ink-off-acrylic.135641/).
