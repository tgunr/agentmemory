### 🤖 Assistant

Got it — hardwood lumber terminology can look cryptic at first, but it’s actually pretty systematic once you break it down. I’ll explain the common terms and what they’re used for.

### Core Idea: Rough vs Surfaced Lumber
Hardwood lumber is sold either:
- **Rough (rough sawn)** → straight from the mill, not planed smooth  
- **Surfaced (S2S, S4S, etc.)** → milled to be smooth and more uniform

---

### What “S” Means
“S” = **Surfaced** (planed smooth)

Each number tells you how many faces/edges have been milled.

---

### Common Terms Explained

#### 1. **S2S (Surfaced Two Sides)**
- Top and bottom faces are planed smooth
- Edges are still rough

**Use:**
- When you want flat boards but will cut/joint edges yourself
- Common for furniture makers who want control over final width

---

#### 2. **S3S (Surfaced Three Sides)**
- Two faces planed + one edge straightened (jointed)

**Use:**
- Easier to work with than S2S
- Good when you want one straight reference edge for ripping

---

#### 3. **S4S (Surfaced

Four Sides)**
- Both faces + both edges are milled smooth and square
- Ready to use

**Use:**
- DIY projects
- Trim, shelving, quick builds
- When you don’t have jointer/planer tools

---

### Variations You Mentioned

#### **S2S1E**
- Surfaced 2 sides + 1 edge straightened

**Use:**
- Very common in hardwood dealers
- You get flat faces and one true edge to reference on a table saw

---

#### **S4S (sometimes written as 4S or 4S4 — though “4S4” isn’t standard)**
- Proper term is **S4S**
- Fully finished on all sides

---

### Other Important Terms

#### **Rough (Rough Sawn)**
- No surfacing at all
- Cheapest option

**Use:**
- Professional shops
- Maximum thickness control
- Custom milling

---

#### **Hit & Miss (Hit-and-Miss Planed)**
- Light planing just to flatten high spots
- Not uniform thickness

**Use:**
- Rustic furniture
- Keeps rough look but somewhat usable

---

#### **Straight Line Rip (SLR or SL1E)**
- One edge is cut straight on a saw (not jointed)

**Use:**
- Similar to S2S1E but faste

r/cheaper edge prep

---

#### **KD (Kiln Dried) vs AD (Air Dried)**
- **KD**: dried in kiln → stable, indoor use
- **AD**: dried naturally → may move more

---

#### **Nominal vs Actual Size**
- Example: “4/4 lumber”
  - Means **1 inch rough thickness**
  - After surfacing → ~3/4"

Other thicknesses:
- 4/4 = ~1"
- 5/4 = ~1.25"
- 6/4 = ~1.5"
- 8/4 = ~2"

---

### Quick Cheat Sheet

- Rough → cheapest, needs full milling  
- S2S → flat faces, rough edges  
- S2S1E → flat + one straight edge (very practical)  
- S3S → flat + one finished edge  
- S4S → fully finished, ready to use  

---

### When to Choose What

- **Beginner / no big tools** → S4S  
- **Some tools (table saw)** → S2S1E  
- **Full shop (jointer + planer)** → Rough  
- **Furniture making** → Often S2S or rough for control  
- **Quick projects / trim** → S4S

### 🤖 Assistant

Nice — grading is the other half of understanding hardwood lumber, and it’s actually more about **clear usable wood** than overall board appearance.

In the U.S., hardwood grading is set by the **NHLA (National Hardwood Lumber Association)**.

---

## The Big Idea
Grades are based on:
- How much **clear (defect-free) wood** you can cut from a board  
- Not how pretty the whole board looks

This is important: a board can look ugly but still be a **high grade** if it yields large clear sections.
