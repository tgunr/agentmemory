### 👤 User

Tips for reducing charring, I have text engraved with 44w laser running 35% power at 12000 mm/min and it looks good except for slight charring. How to reduce or eliminate to get clean engrave.

---

### 🤖 Assistant

Achieving a clean, char-free engraving with a high-power 44W laser requires balancing heat management and debris removal. At 12,000 mm/min and 35% power, you are already moving quite fast, which is good for minimizing heat soak, but there are several physical and software adjustments you can make to clean up the results.

#### Air Assist Optimization
The most critical factor in reducing charring is your air assist. For engraving, you want a constant, high-pressure stream of air directed exactly at the point of contact. This blows away the smoke and vaporized material before it can settle and bake onto the surrounding surface. If your air assist is weak or poorly aimed, the "soot" will stain the wood grain.

#### Masking Tape
Applying a layer of high-quality paper masking tape (often called transfer tape) over the wood surface before engraving is the most effective "cheat code" for clean results. The laser burns through the tape and into the wood, but any smoke or resin blowback lands on the tape instead of

the wood. Once the job is done, you simply peel the tape off to reveal a perfectly clean engraving.

#### Power and Speed Balancing
While 12,000 mm/min is fast, a 44W laser is very powerful. You might try increasing the speed even further if your machine's motion system can handle it without losing accuracy. Alternatively, try dropping the power to 25-30% and performing two very fast passes instead of one. This allows the wood to cool slightly between passes, reducing the "scorched" look.

#### Focus and Interval Settings
Ensure your laser is perfectly focused. An out-of-focus beam is wider and less efficient, which creates more radiant heat and more charring. Additionally, check your "Line Interval" or "Lines per mm" in your software (like LightBurn). If the lines are too close together, you are effectively re-burning the same area multiple times, which increases charring. A setting of 0.08mm to 0.1mm is usually a good starting point for text.

#### Material Preparation
The type of wood matters

significantly. Oily or resinous woods like pine or cedar char much more easily than hardwoods like maple or cherry. If you are working with plywood, the glue layers can also contribute to soot. Lightly sanding the surface with 400-grit sandpaper after engraving can often remove light surface charring without damaging the depth of the text.

#### Software "Overscanning"
Ensure "Overscanning" is enabled in your laser software. This allows the laser head to continue moving past the edge of the design before slowing down and changing direction. Without overscanning, the laser dwells for a fraction of a second longer at the edges of the letters as it decelerates, which often causes darker charring on the left and right boundaries of your text.
