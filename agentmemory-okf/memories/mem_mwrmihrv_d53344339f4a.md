There are a few ways, depending on what you want to do:

## 1. Continuity Camera (built into macOS, the usual answer)
Use your iPad as a wireless webcam for Mac apps (FaceTime, Zoom, QuickTime, etc.).

**Requirements:**
- Mac on macOS Ventura (13) or later, iPad on iPadOS 16+
- Both signed into the **same Apple ID** with **two-factor auth** on
- **Wi-Fi and Bluetooth on** for both, within ~30 ft
- iPad locked/unattended (you don't need to unlock it)

**How:**
1. In any camera app on the Mac (FaceTime, Zoom, Photo Booth, etc.), open the camera/video source menu.
2. Pick your **iPad** from the list.
3. It connects automatically. Prop the iPad in landscape with the rear camera facing your subject.

> Note: Apple's Continuity Camera webcam feature is officially marketed for **iPhone**, but **iPad also appears as a source** on current macOS versions. If it doesn't show up, use one of the options below.

## 2. Capture photos/scans directly (Continuity Camera — Insert from iPad)
In Finder, Notes, Mail, Pages, etc.:
1. Right-click (or Edit menu) → **Import from iPhone or iPad** → **Take Photo** or **Scan Documents**.
2. The iPad camera opens; the shot drops straight into your Mac document.

## 3. Wired / live preview via QuickTime
1. Connect iPad to Mac with a **USB-C/Lightning cable**.
2. Open **QuickTime Player** → **File → New Movie Recording**.
3. Click the arrow next to the record button → select your **iPad** as the camera source.
4. You get a live preview and can record.

## 4. Third-party apps (most flexible, for odd cases)
If the built-in route won't expose the iPad as a generic webcam for a specific app:
- **Camo** (Reincubate) — turns iPad/iPhone into a high-quality webcam, wired or wireless, with controls for focus/exposure.
- **EpocCam**, **iVCam** — similar.

---

**Quick pick:**
- Just need it as a webcam → **Continuity Camera** (option 1), no cable.
- Grabbing a photo/doc scan into a file → **option 2**.
- Recording or a reliable wired feed → **QuickTime** (option 3) or **Camo** (option 4).
