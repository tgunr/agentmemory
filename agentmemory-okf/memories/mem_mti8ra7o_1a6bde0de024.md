### Troubleshooting a John Deere 2432R With Very Hard Steering (No Assist)

Since your hydraulic fluid level is OK and the steering feels like manual-only, focus on issues that block or starve hydraulic flow to the steering circuit, or a failure in the steering valve/assist cylinder. Work through the checks below in order—many are quick and can pinpoint the fault without disassembly.

#### Safety first
- Park on level ground, engine off, key removed.
- Support the front end safely if you’ll be turning wheels by hand.
- Clean around hydraulic fittings before cracking anything open to avoid contamination.

#### 1) Quick external checks
- Front axle/kingpins and tie-rod ends: Grease points and check for binding. If it’s equally hard to steer with engine off or on, mechanical binding could be the cause.
- Front tire pressure: Low PSI makes steering feel heavy; set to the spec on the tire/owner’s manual.
- Loader weight/ballast: Excess front weight exaggerates symptoms; remove bucket/load for testing.

#### 2)

Listen and feel test (engine running)
- Start the tractor, idle to mid-RPM.
- Slowly turn the wheel lock-to-lock while listening near the steering column and under the hood.
  - If you hear the pump whine change as you turn, hydraulic flow is attempting to build—steering valve may be bypassing internally or assist cylinder may be leaking internally.
  - If there’s no change in pump sound at all, the steering metering/priority valve may not be getting priority flow, or the pump isn’t supplying.

#### 3) Check hydraulic filters and suction path
- Main hydraulic filter: If recently changed, confirm the correct filter and no collapsed media. A clogged filter starves the system and commonly causes “no assist” symptoms.
- Suction screen/strainer (if your model has one in the sump): Remove and clean; debris here will starve the pump.
- Look for aeration:
  - Milky fluid or foaming indicates air ingestion—often cracked suction hose, loose clamps, or bad O-rings on the suction side. Air in the oil will kill assist

first.

#### 4) Priority/steering supply line issues
Many Deere compact/utility tractors send pump flow to a priority (steering) valve before the rest of the hydraulic system.
- Inspect hoses from pump to priority valve and from priority valve to steering hand metering unit:
  - Kinks, crushed sections, or quick-couplers not fully seated (if routed through a loader valve) can block flow.
  - If you have a front-end loader, make sure the power-beyond/return lines are connected correctly. An incorrectly reconnected loader valve can rob or block steering flow.

Tip: If your steering quit right after a loader removal/reinstall or hydraulic service, recheck those couplers. A single partially seated coupler can give you exactly these symptoms.

#### 5) Steering hand metering unit (orbitrol) test
- With front wheels off the ground, engine running at idle, turn the wheel:
  - If you feel intermittent “notchy” pulses, the orbitrol may be starved or internally bypassing.
  - If you remove the return hose briefly

(catch in a container) and turning the wheel produces little to no flow, the metering unit may not be receiving supply (priority issue) or is failed.
- Check column/shaft for play or sheared coupler between steering wheel and metering unit.

Note: Only disconnect lines if you’re comfortable and have caps/plugs and spill control. Keep everything clean.
