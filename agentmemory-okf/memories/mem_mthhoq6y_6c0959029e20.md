How to remove skin from salmon

---

### 🤖 Assistant — 2025-03-21T23:21:22Z

To remove the skin from salmon, follow these steps:

1. **Prepare Your Tools**: You'll need a sharp fillet knife or a chef’s knife, a cutting board, and optionally a pair of pliers or tweezers for pin bones. Place a damp paper towel under the cutting board to keep it from slipping.

2. **Start with a Cut**: Place the salmon fillet skin-side down on the cutting board. Make a small incision between the flesh and the skin at one end (usually the tail end) to create a tab of skin you can grip. Angle the knife slightly downward toward the skin to avoid cutting into the flesh.

3. **Grip the Skin**: Hold the skin tab with your non-dominant hand (you can use a paper towel for better grip if it’s slippery). Keep the fish steady.

4. **Slide the Knife**: With your dominant hand, slide the knife along the length of the fillet, keeping it close to the skin. Use a gentle sawing motion if needed, letting the knife do the work while pulling the skin slightly in the opposite direction. The goal is to separate the flesh from the skin without removing too much meat.

5. **Check for Bones**: Once the skin is off, run your fingers along the fillet to feel for pin bones. Remove them with tweezers or pliers if present.

6. **Trim if Needed**: Trim any remaining bits of skin or fat for a clean fillet.

Tips: Work with a cold fillet (straight from the fridge) as it’s firmer and easier to handle. If the skin resists, adjust the knife angle and go slow to avoid tearing the flesh.

That’s it—your salmon is now skinless and ready to cook!

### 🤖 Assistant — 2025-03-21T23:21:56Z

Here’s an alternative method to remove the skin from salmon using hot water, which can make the process easier, especially if you’re not confident with a knife:

1. **Boil Water**: Bring a pot of water to a boil, then reduce it to a simmer (just below boiling, around 180-190°F or 80-85°C). You don’t want to cook the salmon, just loosen the skin.

2. **Prepare the Salmon**: Place the salmon fillet on a cutting board or a heat-safe surface, skin-side up. No need to cut it yet.

3. **Pour Hot Water**: Carefully pour a small amount of the hot water over the skin—just enough to cover it for a few seconds (about 10-20 seconds). This will loosen the skin without cooking the flesh. You’ll see the skin start to lighten or curl slightly.

4. **Cool Slightly**: Let the fillet sit for a moment so it’s safe to handle, or rinse it briefly with cold water to stop any cooking. Pat it dry with a paper towel.

5. **Peel the Skin**: Starting at one end (like the tail), grip the skin with your fingers or a paper towel and gently peel it back. It should come off easily in one piece. If it sticks in spots, use a knife to carefully lift those areas and continue peeling.

6. **Check and Clean**: Inspect the fillet for any remaining skin patches or pin bones. Trim or remove them as needed with a knife or tweezers.

This method is great if you want to avoid intricate knife work. The heat softens the connective tissue between the skin and flesh, making it almost effortless to peel. Just be cautious not to overdo the hot water exposure to keep the salmon raw for cooking.
