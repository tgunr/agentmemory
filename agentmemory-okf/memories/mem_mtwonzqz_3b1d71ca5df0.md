**UV Resin as seal coat over epoxy:**
- Pros:
  - Very hard, durable surface
  - Crystal clear / high gloss
  - Fast cure (seconds to minutes under UV light)
  - Can be built up in layers
  - Chemical resistant when fully cured
  - Good for coating epoxy to add an extra hard, glossy layer
  
- Cons:
  - Can yellow over time with UV exposure (some formulations)
  - Requires UV light source (lamp/sun)
  - Can have compatibility issues with epoxy - some epoxy amines can inhibit UV cure
  - Temperature sensitive during cure
  - Can be brittle/craze over flexible substrates
  - Oxygen inhibition on surface (sticky layer unless post-cured)
  - Thickness control is tricky - too thick and it doesn't cure properly
  - Some types shrink during cure

**Shellac as seal coat over epoxy:**
- Pros:
  - Excellent adhesion to many surfaces including cured epoxy
  - Very easy to apply (brush/spray)
  - Fast drying (minutes)
  - Non-toxic, natural, easy cleanup (denatured alcohol)
  - Good sealer for epoxy prior to other finishes
  - Can be sanded and recoated easily
  - Low cost, widely available
  - Good at sealing epoxy pores/porosity before applying other coatings
  - Traditional finish, well-understood behavior
  
- Cons:
  - Not very durable compared to modern finishes
  - Can be damaged by water/alcohol (white rings)
  - Not heat resistant
  - Can darken/yellow over time (especially blonde shellac to some degree)
  - Only moderately chemical resistant
  - Requires reapplication if damaged
  - Not as hard as UV resin or polyurethane
  - UV sensitive - fades in direct sunlight
