### 🤖 Assistant

Southern-style collard greens are a staple of soul food, known for being slow-simmered until tender in a savory, smoky broth often called "pot liquor." This recipe uses smoked meat for depth and a touch of heat and acidity to balance the richness.

### Southern-Style Collard Greens

#### Ingredients
To make a traditional batch, you will need: 2 large bunches of fresh collard greens (about 2-3 pounds), 1 tablespoon of olive oil or bacon drippings, 1 large yellow onion (diced), 3 cloves of garlic (minced), 1 smoked ham hock or 2-3 strips of thick-cut smoked bacon, 4 cups of chicken broth or water, 2 tablespoons of apple cider vinegar, 1 tablespoon of brown sugar, 1 teaspoon of red pepper flakes, and salt and black pepper to taste.

#### Preparation
Start by thoroughly washing the collard greens in a sink full of cool water to remove any grit or sand; you may need to do this multiple times. Remove the tough woody stems by folding the leaf in half and pulling the stem away, or by cutting along the rib with a

knife. Stack several leaves, roll them up tightly like a cigar, and slice them into 1-inch wide ribbons.

#### Cooking Instructions
In a large pot or Dutch oven, heat the oil or bacon drippings over medium heat. Add the diced onion and cook until softened and translucent, about 5 minutes. Stir in the minced garlic and red pepper flakes, cooking for another minute until fragrant.

Add the smoked ham hock or bacon to the pot along with the chicken broth, apple cider vinegar, and brown sugar. Bring the liquid to a boil, then reduce the heat to low, cover, and simmer for about 20-30 minutes to allow the smoky flavor to infuse the broth.

Begin adding the collard greens to the pot in batches, stirring them down as they wilt to make room for more. Once all the greens are in, cover the pot and simmer on low heat. Cook the greens for 1 to 1.5 hours, or until they reach your desired level of tenderness.

#### Finishing Touches
Once the greens are tender, remove the ham hock or bacon. If using a ham hock, you can pull

the meat off the bone, chop it up, and stir it back into the greens. Taste the broth and adjust the seasoning with salt, pepper, or an extra splash of vinegar if needed. Serve the greens hot, ideally with a side of cornbread to soak up the flavorful pot liquor.
