This is one of the most common Telegram spam patterns out there — you're not imagining it, and it's not personal. Here's what's actually going on:

## It's automated scam campaigns, not real people

- **Mass bot messaging**: Scammers run bots that blast the same opening lines to thousands of Telegram accounts. The "Asian woman" framing is a template — they rotate through different personas (Russian, Asian, Latin American, etc.) depending on the campaign.
- **Goal**: Get you to reply. Once you engage, the script escalates — usually toward crypto/"investment" pitches, fake romance, or malware links.
- **Not targeted at you specifically**: You're just in a list they're spraying. Same message goes to tens of thousands of users.

## Why Telegram specifically

- Telegram's API makes bulk messaging trivial to automate.
- Less moderation/account-takedown pressure than platforms like WhatsApp or iMessage.
- Usernames/usernames are easy to scrape and target.

## The term "oriental"

Just a heads-up — "oriental" is an outdated term for people that most folks today find offensive or at least odd. It persists in some places (older English usage, some product labels) but for people it's generally avoided now. Worth knowing if you're ever wondering why a message might read as stilted or off.

## What actually helps

1. **Don't reply at all.** Zero engagement. Replying confirms your account is active and watched.
2. **Block + report** the account in Telegram.
3. **Privacy settings**: Set your Telegram to "Who can find me by phone number" → Nobody, and "Who can add me to groups/channels" → My Contacts. This cuts down inbound spam a lot.
4. **Don't click links** from strangers, obviously.
5. If it's really relentless, a username change can help — some spam lists are built from old/known handles.

Bottom line: you're getting sprayed by a scam operation, not being singled out. The safest move is total non-engagement, and tightening privacy a bit.
