**Three erectile columns**

- **Corpora cavernosa** (×2, paired, dorsal) — spongy erectile tissue of endothelial-lined sinusoids and trabeculae of smooth muscle and fibrous tissue. Joined in the midline by an incomplete **septum pectiniforme** (perforated, allowing communication), and proximally diverge as the **crura**, which attach to the ischiopubic rami.
- **Corpus spongiosum** (single, ventral) — expands proximally as the **bulb** (fixed to the perineal membrane) and distally as the **glans penis**; contains the penile urethra.
- **Glans** — corona glandis, external urethral orifice, frenulum; covered by prepuce (if uncircumcised).

**Fibrous/fascial envelopes (inside → out)**

1. **Tunica albuginea** — dense fibrous sheath; thick around the corpora cavernosa, thin around the spongiosum (why the spongiosum stays compressible).
2. Buck's fascia (deep fascia of the penis) — encloses all three columns plus the deep dorsal vessels and dorsal nerves.
3. Loose areolar layer, then **Dartos fascia** (continuous with Colles' fascia and Scarpa's), then skin.
4. Supporting ligaments: **suspensory** (deep, from pubic symphysis) and **fundiform** (superficial, from linea alba).

**Urethra**

Spongy (penile) urethra runs the length of the corpus spongiosum; navicular fossa just proximal to the meatus. Proximally it becomes bulbar and membranous urethra.

**Vascular supply**

- Arteries: internal pudendal → common penile → **cavernosal (deep) arteries** (give off coiled **helicine arteries** into the sinusoids), **dorsal artery of penis**, and bulbourethral artery.
- Venous drainage: superficial dorsal vein → external pudendal → great sap
