Here’s a classic French onion soup recipe that’s rich, flavorful, and perfect for a cozy meal. This version serves about 4 people and takes roughly 1–1.5 hours to prepare and cook.

### Ingredients
- **Onions**: 2 lbs (about 6 medium) yellow onions, thinly sliced
- **Butter**: 4 tablespoons unsalted
- **Olive oil**: 1 tablespoon
- **Garlic**: 2 cloves, minced
- **Beef broth**: 6 cups (preferably low-sodium, high-quality stock)
- **Dry white wine**: ½ cup (optional, but adds depth—e.g., Sauvignon Blanc)
- **Thyme**: 2–3 sprigs fresh (or ½ teaspoon dried)
- **Bay leaf**: 1
- **Salt**: To taste
- **Black pepper**: Freshly ground, to taste
- **Baguette**: 4–8 slices (depending on bowl size), toasted
- **Gruyère cheese**: 1½ cups grated (or a mix with Swiss if you prefer)

### Instructions
1. **Caramelize the Onions**  
   - Heat butter and olive oil in a large pot or Dutch oven over medium heat.  
   - Add sliced onions and a pinch of salt. Cook, stirring occasionally, for 35–45 minutes until they’re deeply golden brown and caramelized. (Patience is key—don’t rush this!) If they stick, add a splash of water to deglaze.  
   - Stir in minced garlic during the last 2 minutes.

2. **Deglaze and Simmer**  
   - Pour in the white wine (if using) and scrape up any browned bits from the pot. Let it simmer for 2–3 minutes until mostly evaporated.  
   - Add beef broth, thyme, and bay leaf. Bring to a boil, then reduce to a simmer. Cook uncovered for 20–30 minutes to let the flavors meld.  
   - Season with salt and pepper to taste. Remove thyme sprigs and bay leaf.

3. **Prepare the Topping**  
   - Preheat your oven’s broiler.  
   - Ladle soup into oven-safe bowls. Place a toasted baguette slice (or two) on top of each.  
   - Generously sprinkle Gruyère cheese over the bread.  

4. **Broil**  
   - Place bowls on a baking sheet and broil for 2–5 minutes, until the cheese is melted, bubbly, and slightly browned. Watch closely to avoid burning!

5. **Serve**  
   - Let cool slightly (it’ll be very hot) and enjoy with a spoon and a big appetite.

### Tips
- **Onion slicing**: Use a sharp knife or mandoline for thin, even slices—they’ll cook down better.
- **Broth**: Homemade beef stock elevates this, but a good store-bought one works too.
- **Make ahead**: The soup base (without bread and cheese) tastes even better the next day.
