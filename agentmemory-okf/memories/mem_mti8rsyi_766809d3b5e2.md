### 👤 User

On an RV trailer tongue lift that is electric 12 volt DC, it is not working when the system is only on battery, or when it's plugged into the vehicle or the utility power is applied it works. What could be the problem? Noises here, but no movement.

---

### 🤖 Assistant

It sounds like your electric tongue jack isn’t getting enough voltage/current from the battery alone, but it works when the vehicle/shore power is supplying extra power. “Noise but no movement” is classic low-voltage or high-resistance symptom. Here’s a concise checklist to find and fix it:

Likeliest causes
- Weak or sulfated battery: Battery voltage sags under load; the jack motor hums but can’t turn.
- Bad ground connection: Corroded, loose, or paint-blocked ground from jack to trailer frame or from battery negative to frame.
- High-resistance power path: Corroded lugs, undersized/aged inline fuse holder, failing resettable breaker, bad switch/relay, or crusty quick-disconnect.
- Partially seized jack or dry screw: Mechanical load too high for the available battery current, but passes when alternator/shore converter boosts voltage.
- Failing motor or capacitor (if equipped): Draws excessive current, only runs with boosted voltage.

Step-by-step tests (quick)
1) Measure battery at rest:
- >12.6 V = fully

charged; 12.2–12.4 V = low; <12.0 V = discharged.
2) Measure battery under load (while pressing jack switch):
- If it drops below ~10.5–11.0 V, the battery is weak or there’s a big resistance in the circuit.
3) Measure voltage at the jack leads under load:
- If battery holds ~12+ V but jack sees much less, you have wiring/fuse/ground resistance.
- If jack sees ~12 V and still won’t move, suspect motor/capacitor/mechanical bind.

Common fixes
- Clean and tighten all connections:
  - Battery posts and clamps (remove, wire-brush, reattach).
  - Ground strap from battery negative to frame (remove paint/rust, star washer, dielectric grease).
  - Jack ground to frame (often its mounting bolts are the ground—clean to bare metal).
  - Positive feed lugs, butt splices, ring terminals.
- Inspect/replace protection devices:
  - Inline fuse or automatic reset breaker near battery. Old fuse holders can cause big voltage drop; replace with a new weatherproof holder and correct fuse/breaker size listed by the jack

manufacturer.
- Check wire gauge and length:
  - Long skinny wires cause voltage drop. If someone replaced with thinner wire, upgrade to proper gauge (often 8–10 AWG for jacks).
- Test the battery:
  - Fully charge with a smart charger; then load-test. Replace if it fails or is older/sulfated.
- Lube the jack:
  - Follow the manufacturer procedure: clean, lightly grease the screw/gear (some have a zerk or specific top-cap access). Try running it with the trailer weight off (use blocks) to see if it frees up.
- Try the manual crank:
  - If very stiff, mechanical binding is likely. If it turns freely by hand but not on battery, it’s electrical.
- Check the switch and internal relay:
  - If external wiring is good but voltage still drops across the switch, the switch/relay has high resistance—replace.
- Motor/capacitor:
  - Some jacks have a start/run capacitor under the head cover. A failed cap can cause humming/no start; replace with same spec.

What to look for in readings
- Battery at rest ≥12.5 V, and

≥11.0 V at the jack while lifting, is typical. If shore power/vehicle connected raises system voltage to ~13.6–14.4 V and the jack works, that strongly points to either a weak battery or resistance in the wiring/ground.

- Jack brand/model,
I can pinpoint whether it’s the battery, ground, fuse holder, or the jack head (switch/cap/motor).
