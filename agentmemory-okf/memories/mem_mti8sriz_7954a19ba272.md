### 👤 User

I live in Montgomery County, Texas, and I have a friend (supposedly friend) that I let park behind my barn with his trailer, along with his wife. The original agreement was that I would provide the spot and hook him up to electricity, water, and sewer for $400 a month as long as he helped out around the property. He now has a full-time job and doesn't seem to have any time to do anything on the property. I have communicated this and he said that he would take care of things. But nothing is happening. His communication skills are very poor. He constantly avoids communicating with me. And I warned him in the last conversation that if I had to discuss this one more time, I was going to ask him to leave. What legal recourse do I have to evict him, being as we basically only have a verbal agreement?

### 🤖 Assistant

You’re in a pretty common (and frustrating) situation, and Texas law actually gives you a clear path—even with just a verbal agreement.

### First: What your situation likely is (legally)
Even though it’s informal, this is probably considered a **landlord–tenant relationship**, not just “letting a friend park.”  
Why:
- He pays you monthly ($400)
- He lives there (trailer = residence)
- You provide utilities

That means **Texas eviction laws apply**, even without a written lease. A verbal agreement is still enforceable.

---

### Step-by-step: How to legally remove him in Montgomery County

#### 1. Give proper written notice to vacate
You **must** give a written notice before filing eviction.

- Standard notice in Texas: **3 days to vacate**
- You can give longer, but not shorter (unless agreed otherwise—which you didn’t in writing)

Include:
- His name
- Address/location description (e.g., “trailer behind barn at [your address]”)
- Statement that tenancy is terminated
- Deadline (date/time to leave)
- Your

signature/date

Delivery methods:
- Hand delivery (preferably with a witness)
- Tape it to the door of the trailer
- Mail (certified is safest, but not required)

---

#### 2. Wait the notice period
If he leaves → done.  
If not → move to eviction filing.

---

#### 3. File eviction (Forcible Detainer)
File at the **Justice of the Peace court** in your precinct in Montgomery County.

- Filing fee: usually ~$100–$150
- You’ll list:
  - His name
  - Property description
  - Reason: noncompliance (failure to uphold agreement / unpaid labor portion)

---

#### 4. Court hearing
- Usually scheduled within ~1–2 weeks
- Bring:
  - Proof of payments (if any)
  - Text messages / communication showing agreement
  - Notes about the “help around property” condition
  - Copy of your notice to vacate

If you win:
- He typically gets **5 days to move out**

---

#### 5. If he still doesn’t leave
You request a **Writ of Possession**, and the constable will physically remove him.

---

### Important things NOT to do
Do

**not**:
- Shut off utilities
- Tow his trailer yourself
- Change locks or block access
- Remove his belongings

That’s considered **illegal eviction** in Texas and can backfire hard.

---

### About the “help around the property” part
That’s the tricky part—it’s vague and hard to enforce.

But you **don’t actually need that** to evict him.

Since it’s a **month-to-month tenancy**, you can simply:
- Terminate the arrangement for any non-discriminatory reason
- You don’t need to “prove” he didn’t help enough

---

### Practical tip (this matters)
Given his avoidance, don’t keep negotiating verbally.

Just move to:
> “This is formal notice to vacate within 3 days.”

Clean, documented, done.

---

### Optional: A smoother approach
If you want to avoid court drama, you could offer:
- “Leave by X date and I won’t file eviction”

But based on what you described, you may be past that point.
