---

### 🤖 Assistant — 2025-07-28T23:04:07Z

Yes, if you withdraw your entire 401(k) at age 64 and have 20% withheld for taxes, you'll receive the remaining 80% as cash that you can reinvest however you choose. However, there are several important considerations:

**Tax implications:**
- You'll owe ordinary income tax on the full withdrawal amount for that tax year
- The 20% withholding may not cover your entire tax liability, especially if it pushes you into a higher tax bracket
- You could face a significant tax bill when you file your return

**No early withdrawal penalty:**
Since you're over 59½, you won't face the 10% early withdrawal penalty.

**Investment flexibility:**
The cash you receive can be invested in any way you want - taxable brokerage accounts, real estate, bonds, CDs, or other investments. You're not restricted to retirement accounts.

**Loss of tax advantages:**
Once withdrawn, that money loses the tax-deferred growth benefits of the 401(k). Future gains in taxable accounts will be subject to capital gains taxes.

**Consider alternatives:**
- Rolling over to an IRA maintains tax-deferred status
- Taking partial withdrawals over multiple years could help manage tax brackets
- Converting to a Roth IRA (though this also triggers taxes)

You might want to consult with a tax professional or financial advisor to model the tax impact and discuss whether this strategy aligns with your overall retirement and investment goals.
