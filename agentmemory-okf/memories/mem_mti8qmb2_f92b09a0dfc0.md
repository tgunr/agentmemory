### 👤 User

How much information can be stored in a strand of dna

---

### 🤖 Assistant

DNA is one of the most efficient and dense information storage mediums known to science. While exact figures vary depending on the coding method used by different research teams, the storage capacity of DNA is staggering when compared to traditional silicon-based hardware.

### Storage Capacity and Density
The amount of data that can be stored in DNA is typically measured by weight rather than by a single strand, as many thousands of tiny strands usually make up a readable sample.

*   **Per Gram:** Scientists estimate that **one gram of DNA** can store between **215 petabytes (215 million gigabytes)** and **455 exabytes** of data. For context, 215 petabytes is enough to store roughly 36 million copies of a high-definition movie like *Avengers: Endgame* in a single gram of material.
*   **Per Nucleotide:** Digital data is stored in the four bases (A, C, G, and T). While a theoretical limit exists at **2 bits per nucleotide**, practical experiments (using error-correction algorithms) have achieved densities

of about **1.6 bits per nucleotide**.
*   **Global Scale:** Researchers estimate that all the digital data currently in the world (roughly 175 zettabytes expected by 2025) could theoretically be stored in about **81 kilograms of DNA**.

### Why DNA is Used for Storage
Traditional storage media like hard drives or CDs eventually degrade and become obsolete. DNA offers several unique advantages:

*   **Extreme Longevity:** If kept in a cool, dry place, DNA can remain stable for thousands of years. Scientists have successfully sequenced DNA from fossils that are over **700,000 years old**.
*   **High Energy Efficiency:** Storing DNA requires very little energy once written, essentially only needing enough energy to maintain a cold storage environment.
*   **Future-Proofing:** Unlike floppy disks or Zip drives, the technology to read DNA (sequencing) will never become obsolete as long as humans are biological beings studying their own genetics.

### Current Limitations
While the density is incredible, the

technology is not yet ready for consumer use for two main reasons:
1.  **Cost:** It is currently very expensive to "write" (synthesize) and "read" (sequence) DNA. Encoding just 2 megabytes of data can cost several thousand dollars.
2.  **Speed:** Accessing data in DNA is slow. It is currently being targeted as a "cold storage" solution—meant for archival data that needs to be preserved for centuries rather than files that need to be accessed instantly.

Sources include [Wyss Institute](https://wyss.harvard.edu/news/save-it-in-dna/), [Science.org](https://www.science.org/content/article/dna-could-store-all-worlds-data-one-room), and [Electropages](https://www.electropages.com/blog/2020/08/storing-information-and-data-dna).
