### 🤖 Assistant — 2026-08-30T04:30:05Z

I’ll look up proven ways to restore gloss on a clear epoxy flood coat and whether PlastX is the right product for that finish.PlastX is the wrong tool for matching a fresh flood-coat pour. It is a mild headlight/plexiglass polish. It can clean light haze and very fine scratches by hand, but it does not level the micro-scratch pattern that makes a used epoxy top look dull instead of wet-glass.

A new pour is glossy because it self-levels into an optically flat surface. Everyday wear, cleaning, and even dust create a fine scratch network that scatters light. Polish can get close. A thin recoat is what actually recreates the original look.

### 1. Try a real compound/polish first (no new epoxy)
Do this if a fingernail does not catch in scratches and the surface is just dull or lightly hazed.

1. Clean with mild dish soap and water. Dry. Wipe with isopropyl alcohol or denatured alcohol (not Windex, not mineral spirits).
2. Switch products:
   - Cutting step if needed: Meguiar’s **M105** Ultra Cut Compound
   - Finish step: Meguiar’s **M205** Ultra Finishing Polish  
   Novus 2 or Flitz paste also work well on epoxy.
3. Use a dual-action / orbital polisher with a foam pad if you can. Hand-rubbing PlastX on a large flat top almost never matches machine work.
4. Work small sections, wipe residue with a clean microfiber, then inspect in raking light.

That combo is what epoxy table makers actually use after wet-sanding. PlastX is a step below it.

### 2. Wet-sand then polish (closest without recoating)
If polish alone still looks short of the original pour:

- Wet-sand the whole top: **1500 → 2000 → 3000** grit (keep paper and surface wet).
- Compound with M105, then finish with M205.
- Optional last step: a thin coat of paste wax or a plastic-safe sealant.

You will get a very high gloss. It still will not be quite as “liquid” as a fresh self-leveled pour, because you are polishing a sanded surface rather than letting resin level itself.

### 3. Thin flood coat (true original gloss)
This is the reliable reset when polish is not enough.

1. Sand the entire surface with **220 grit** until it is uniformly dull — no shiny islands.
2. Vacuum thoroughly, then wipe with acetone or denatured alcohol. Let it flash dry.
3. Pour a thin table-top / bar-top flood coat (about 1/8 in / 3 mm or less). Same class of epoxy you used originally.
4. Spread, then pass a heat gun or propane torch quickly to pop bubbles.
5. Let it fully cure in a clean, dust-free space.

The new layer fills micro-scratches and self-levels the same way the first pour did. That is why it looks original again.

Do not skip the sanding step. New epoxy will not bond reliably to a glossy, unsanded flood coat.

### After you get the gloss back
- Clean with mild soap and water only.
- Avoid ammonia glass cleaners, abrasive pads, and harsh solvents.
- Use coasters and felt pads. Hot pans and sliding objects are what dull epoxy fastest.
- A light polish every so often maintains it; recoating is only needed when wear is widespread.

If you say whether the top has visible scratches you can feel, or just looks flat/hazy, the starting grit and whether you should skip straight to a recoat can be narrowed further.
