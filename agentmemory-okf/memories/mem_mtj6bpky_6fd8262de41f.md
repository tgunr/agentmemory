A family with $12,000 monthly income and credit scores of 650-700 can definitely pursue homeownership in California, though they'll need to be strategic about location and loan options.

**Income and Debt-to-Income Ratio**
With $144,000 annual income, lenders typically allow housing payments up to 28-31% of gross income, meaning roughly $3,360-3,720 monthly for housing costs (including principal, interest, taxes, insurance, and HOA fees). Having multiple income sources can actually strengthen the application if all providers have stable employment history.

**Loan Options**
Given the credit score range, they'll qualify for conventional loans, though rates may be slightly higher than prime borrowers. FHA loans could be attractive since they accept lower credit scores and require only 3.5% down payment. VA loans would be ideal if any family member is a veteran. USDA loans might work for rural areas in California.

**Down Payment and Location Strategy**
California's median home price makes coastal areas challenging, but inland regions like Central Valley, parts of Riverside County, or northern California offer more affordable options. With a 5% down payment on a $400,000 home (more realistic in these areas), they'd need $20,000 plus closing costs.

**Additional Considerations**
They should shop around with multiple lenders since some specialize in multiple-income households. First-time buyer programs in various California counties offer down payment assistance. Credit score improvement efforts could lower interest rates significantly. Consider getting pre-approved to understand exact purchasing power and strengthen offers in competitive markets.

The key is finding the right location where their income stretches furthest while building equity in California's generally appreciating real estate market.
