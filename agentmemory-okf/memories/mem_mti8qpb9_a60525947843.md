### Recommended Materials for Vacuum Tables

For a vacuum table like the AirWeights system—used in CNC machining for workholding— the choice of material is crucial for balancing strength, machinability, vacuum integrity, and cost. Based on common industry practices for similar vacuum workholding tables (e.g., those used for holding flat stock like wood, composites, or metals during routing or milling), aluminum is widely regarded as the best overall material. Here's why, along with alternatives:

#### Why Aluminum?
- **Strength and Rigidity**: Aluminum alloys (such as 6061 or 7075) provide excellent stiffness-to-weight ratio, ensuring the table remains flat and resists deformation under vacuum pressure or machining loads. It's lightweight, which makes it easier to handle and integrate into CNC setups.
- **Machinability**: It machines cleanly for creating the grid of vacuum channels, ports, and mounting holes without excessive tool wear. Anodizing (a common finish) adds corrosion resistance and seals the

surface to prevent leaks.
- **Thermal Properties**: Low thermal expansion helps maintain precision during operations where heat from cutting tools is involved.
- **Cost-Effectiveness**: More affordable than alternatives like stainless steel, while still durable for most shop environments. It's non-magnetic, which is a plus if you're working with magnetic-sensitive materials.
- **Vacuum Performance**: Aluminum's smooth surface finish supports good sealing with gaskets or pods, and it's easy to integrate manifolds for even vacuum distribution.

Many commercial vacuum tables, including those similar to AirWeights, use anodized aluminum for these reasons. If you're building or customizing one, start with 6061-T6 aluminum plate (about 1/2" to 1" thick) for a 24x24" table—it's readily available and CNC-friendly.

#### Alternatives to Consider
- **Stainless Steel (e.g., 304 or 316)**: Opt for this if corrosion resistance is critical (e.g., in humid or chemical-exposed environments). It's stronger and more durable

than aluminum but heavier, more expensive, and harder to machine, which could increase fabrication costs. Good for high-vacuum applications but overkill for standard CNC workholding.
- **Acrylic or Polycarbonate (for Low-Load Applications)**: These plastics are transparent (helpful for visibility), lightweight, and cheap, but they're not suitable for heavy-duty CNC due to lower rigidity and potential warping under vacuum or heat. Best for prototyping or light-duty laser cutting.
- **Composite Materials (e.g., Phenolic Resin or MDF with Epoxy Coating)**: Used in budget or woodworking-specific tables. They're flat and affordable but absorb moisture, leading to warping, and lack the precision of metals for metalworking.

#### Key Factors for Your Choice
- **Application**: For general CNC (wood, plastic, light metals), aluminum is ideal. For heavy industrial use or harsh conditions, lean toward stainless.
