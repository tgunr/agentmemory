### Summary of the invention
It describes a self-leveling mechanism, primarily for a book stand that holds thick/heavy volumes (e.g., encyclopedias or dictionaries). When a thick book is opened on a flat surface, the pages often do not lie flat. This device automatically adjusts so the reading surfaces stay level and flat regardless of where the book is opened.<grok:render card_id="cbe80b" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">55</argument></grok:render>

**Key features**:
- A base with a vertical post supporting a cross-brace angled upward.
- A hollow U-tube with parallel legs containing hydraulic fluid.
- Telescoping hollow piston members (with platforms/tables on top) that slide into the U-tube legs.
- Pistons at the bottoms of the sliding members, plus compression springs that counterbalance weight.
- Guide rods and adjustable plates to support the book covers.
- Compressible fluid (air) above the hydraulic fluid level inside the pistons for cushioning.

**How it works**: An unbalanced downward force on one side compresses its spring and displaces hydraulic fluid to the other side, raising the opposite platform by roughly the same amount. Springs progressively balance the forces, and controlled leakage plus air compression provides smooth, cushioned movement. The design improves on earlier mechanical book stands (e.g., those under U.S. Patents 2,260,694 and 2,260,695).<grok:render card_id="7186dc" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">55</argument></grok:render>

The patent notes possible broader uses (e.g., automobiles, trunks, platforms, or floors), though the illustrated embodiment is a book stand.
