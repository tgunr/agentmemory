### 👤 User — 2025-04-06T14:37:01Z

After planting some forage grasses, namely a mixture of cow peas and soybean and wild bird seed, how long should I wait before I allow the cattle and goats to graze upon it?

---

### 🤖 Assistant — 2025-04-06T14:37:10Z

For a mixture of cow peas, soybeans, and wild bird seed as forage crops, you should wait approximately 6-8 weeks after planting before allowing cattle and goats to graze. This timing allows the plants to:

1. Establish strong root systems
2. Develop sufficient leaf area for optimal regrowth after grazing
3. Reach an appropriate height (typically 12-18 inches for legumes like cow peas and soybeans)

Specific considerations for your mix:
- Cow peas and soybeans are typically ready for grazing 45-60 days after planting
- The wild bird seed components may vary in maturity timeframes
- Grazing too early reduces total yield and can damage plants before they're established

For best results, monitor the growth rather than relying solely on calendar days. When plants reach 12-18 inches with good leaf development, they're generally ready for controlled grazing. Consider rotational grazing to maximize forage productivity and prevent overgrazing.

### 👤 User — 2025-04-06T14:37:26Z

What about the same for Bermuda grass?

---

### 🤖 Assistant — 2025-04-06T14:37:32Z

For Bermuda grass, you should wait approximately 6-8 weeks (42-56 days) after planting before allowing cattle and goats to graze. This waiting period is essential for several reasons:

1. Root establishment - Bermuda grass needs time to develop its extensive root system
2. Adequate coverage - You want it to reach about 70-80% ground coverage 
3. Proper height - The grass should be about 6-8 inches tall before first grazing

Some additional considerations for Bermuda grass:

- If you planted from sprigs or plugs rather than seed, you might need the full 8 weeks
- First grazing should be light to moderate to avoid damaging new plants
- Avoid grazing when the soil is very wet as this can damage new plants through trampling
- After the initial grazing, allow the Bermuda grass to regrow to 6-8 inches before grazing again

When managed properly, established Bermuda grass is quite resilient and can handle fairly intensive grazing pressure, but that initial establishment period is crucial for long-term productivity.
