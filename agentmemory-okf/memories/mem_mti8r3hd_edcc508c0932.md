### 👤 User

I'm running a two-surfaced board through my jointer and it appears that the center is more open than the ends. In other words it's bowed. What would be causing this on a jointer?

---

### 🤖 Assistant

I'll help you figure out what's causing that bow on your jointer.

If your jointer is creating a **bow (convex curve)** on your board—where the center is higher than the ends—the most likely causes are:

## **1. Outfeed Table Set Too High** (Most Common)
- If the outfeed table is even slightly higher than the cutterhead at top dead center, the board will be lifted as it exits the cut
- This causes the middle to remain higher while the ends get cut more
- **Fix**: Lower the outfeed table so it's exactly level with the cutterhead knives at their highest point

## **2. Infeed Table Set Too Low**
- An infeed table that's too far below the outfeed creates too aggressive a cut
- Can contribute to uneven material removal
- **Fix**: Check that your infeed/outfeed tables are parallel and adjust depth of cut appropriately

## **3. Improper Feed Pressure/Technique**
- Starting with too much pressure on the infeed side, then not shifting pressure to the outfeed side as the board passes over the cutterhead
- This can cause the board to ride up slightly in the middle
- **Fix**: Start

with pressure on the infeed table, then shift pressure to the outfeed table once enough board has passed the cutterhead

## **4. Cutterhead Knives Not Properly Set**
- Knives set unevenly or not at the correct height relative to the outfeed table
- **Fix**: Use a dial indicator or straightedge to verify knives are exactly at outfeed table height

## **Quick Test**
Place a reliable straightedge across both tables spanning the cutterhead. The outfeed table and the knives at top dead center should just barely kiss the straightedge—no gap, no lifting.

The outfeed table height is almost always the culprit in this scenario.
