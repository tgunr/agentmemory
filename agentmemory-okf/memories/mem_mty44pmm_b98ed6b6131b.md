Very feasible, but with a few gotchas depending on what you're coating.

**As seal coat:** Yes, especially a thin/medium viscosity UV resin. Brush or dab it on to seal layer lines and porous prints before building up thicker top coats. One thin pass, cure under UV, then sand if needed.

**As top coat:** Yes. Thick "casting" or "doming" UV resins work well here — they self-level and give you that hard, glassy gloss. Multiple thin layers beat one thick pour (reduces bubbles, reduces heat/chemical stress).

**Watch out for:**
- **Yellowing** — aromatic UV resins yellow noticeably in sunlight. Use non-aromatic (Ivoclar Vivadent type or "non-yellowing" craft resins) if the piece sees UV.
- **Heat during cure** — thick domes can get hot. Cure in short bursts and let cool between layers if you're doing a thick build.
- **Adhesion to the substrate** — PETG/PLA usually fine, but give the surface a quick scuff first and degrease with IPA. ABS tends to resist.
- **Shrinkage** — UV resins shrink more than epoxy. On large flat pieces, this can pull at edges.
- **Safety** — wear gloves and work in a ventilated area. Uncured resin is skin/eye irritant and sensitizer.

**Workflow that works:** thin seal coat → light sand → 2–3 medium top coats, curing each, then wet-sand/polish to clarity if you need optical quality.
