If I buy ceramic coasters for $37 for 50 coasters and to print 3 coasters is approximately 10 cents per coaster, what should my markup be to sell custom-designed coasters?

---

### 🤖 Assistant

To determine your markup for custom-designed ceramic coasters, you first need to establish your base cost. Based on your purchase of 50 coasters for $37, each blank coaster costs $0.74. Adding the $0.10 printing cost brings your total production cost to $0.84 per coaster.

When selling custom-designed items, a standard retail markup is typically 3x to 4x the production cost to account for your design time, labor, packaging, and platform fees. Here is a breakdown of potential pricing strategies:

#### Pricing Tiers
*   **Standard Retail (3x Markup):** Selling at $2.50 per coaster. This is a common entry point for bulk orders or simple designs, giving you a profit of $1.66 per unit.
*   **Premium/Boutique (4x - 5x Markup):** Selling at $3.50 to $4.25 per coaster. This is more appropriate for highly detailed custom designs, small batches, or if you are selling them in gift sets (e.g., a set of 4 for $15.00).
*   **Wholesale (2x Markup):** Selling at $1.70 per coaster. This is the minimum you should charge if a

local shop wants to carry your items in bulk, as it still covers your costs and a small profit while leaving room for the retailer to add their own markup.

#### Factors to Consider
*   **Design Time:** If a customer wants a completely unique illustration, you should consider adding a one-time "design fee" or "setup fee" (e.g., $15–$25) on top of the per-unit price.
*   **Packaging:** Don't forget to factor in the cost of boxes, bubble wrap, or ribbons if you are selling them as gift sets, as these can easily add another $0.50 to $1.00 to your total cost.
*   **Market Comparison:** Check platforms like Etsy or local craft fairs. Custom ceramic coasters often sell for $5 to $8 each when sold individually, or $20 to $30 for a themed set of four.

Given your low production cost of $0.84, you have a very healthy margin. Aiming for a price point between $3.00 and $5.00 per coaster will keep you competitive while ensuring a strong return on your investment.

### 👤 User

If I'm using a UV printer to make a sticker to put on wood and I peel off the protective layer, do I print a reversed image or a normal image?

---

### 🤖 Assistant

When using a UV printer to create stickers for wood, the orientation of your print depends entirely on **which side of the sticker** will be in contact with the wood.

#### Normal Image (Standard Application)
If you are printing on the **front** of a sticker material (like white vinyl or clear vinyl) and then sticking it onto the wood with the adhesive on the back, you should print a **normal image**. In this case, the protective layer you peel off is likely the backing paper that exposes the adhesive.

#### Reversed Image (Subsurface/Transfer Application)
You only print a **reversed (mirrored) image** if you are using a "UV DTF" (Direct to Film) transfer process or printing on the adhesive side of a clear film. This is common if:
*   You are printing onto a "Film A" (the carrier) where the ink will eventually be flipped over and pressed onto the wood.
*   You want the ink to be sandwiched between the wood and a clear protective top layer.

#### How to Decide
1.  **Check your material:** If you are printing

on the side that **does not** have adhesive, and that side will be facing the viewer once it's on the wood, print **normal**.
2.  **Check the "Peel":** If the "protective layer" you are peeling off is a transfer film that you use to rub the design onto the wood (leaving only the ink behind), you almost always need to print **reversed** so it reads correctly once flipped onto the surface.
