Here’s a straightforward recipe for making bacon at home from pork belly. You can do it with or without smoking, depending on your setup.

---

## Ingredients
- 5 lb (2.25 kg) pork belly, skin on or off (your preference)
- 1/4 cup (50 g) kosher salt
- 2 tsp (5 g) Prague Powder #1 (pink curing salt – very important for safe curing)
- 1/4 cup (50 g) brown sugar (light or dark, depending on flavor preference)
- 2–3 tbsp black pepper (optional)
- 2 tbsp maple syrup or honey (optional, for sweetness)
- Optional flavorings: garlic powder, smoked paprika, chili flakes, bay leaves, etc.

---

## Instructions

### 1. Cure the Pork Belly
1. Mix together the salt, curing salt, sugar, and any optional flavorings.
2. Rub the mixture thoroughly all over the pork belly, coating every surface.
3. Place the pork belly in a large zip-top bag or vacuum-seal bag. Add maple syrup/honey if using.
4. Seal and refrigerate for **7 days**, flipping the bag once daily to redistribute the cure.

---

### 2. Rinse and Rest
1. After curing, remove the pork belly from the bag.
2. Rinse under cold water to remove excess cure.
3. Pat dry with paper towels.
4. Place on a rack in the fridge, uncovered, for **12–24 hours**. This forms a tacky pellicle that helps smoke adhere.

---

### 3. Smoke (or Bake) the Bacon
- **Smoking (traditional):**
  - Smoke at **200°F (93°C)** until the internal temperature reaches **150°F (65°C)**.
  - Wood choice: apple, hickory, cherry, or maple.
- **Oven method:**
  - Bake at **200°F (93°C)** until internal temperature is **150°F (65°C)**.

---

### 4. Chill and Slice
1. Cool the bacon completely, then wrap and refrigerate for several hours.
2. Slice thinly across the grain with a sharp knife or meat slicer.
3. Fry like store-bought bacon.

---

## Storage
- Keeps in the fridge for about **1 week**.
- Freezes well (up to **3 months**) — slice before freezing for convenience.

**Pickling salt** is often used in bacon (and other cures) as a substitute for regular table salt. Here’s why:  

---

## Role of Pickling Salt in Bacon Making
1. **Pure Sodium Chloride (No Additives)**  
   - Pickling salt is just pure salt with **no iodine or anti-caking agents**.  
   - Additives in table salt can give cured meats a **bitter or metallic taste** and may leave cloudy residues in the brine.

2. **Even Dissolving**  
   - It’s ground to a very fine texture, so it dissolves quickly and distributes evenly through the pork belly.  
   - This helps the cure penetrate more consistently.

3. **Curing Efficiency**  
   - Salt’s main role in curing is to **draw out moisture** (via osmosis), making the environment less friendly to bacteria.  
   - It also helps the meat firm up and contributes to the final texture of bacon.

---

## Do You *Need* Pickling Salt?
- Not strictly.  
- **Kosher salt** is the other common choice — but since crystal sizes vary between brands, you should weigh it rather than using cups/spoons.  
- **Table salt** *works* in theory, but its additives and coarser grains make it less desirable.  

---

✅ In short: **Pickling salt just makes curing more predictable and cleaner**, with no extra flavors or residue.
