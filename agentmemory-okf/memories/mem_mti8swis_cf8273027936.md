### 🤖 Assistant

Creating your own bacon rub usually depends on whether you are doing a "dry cure" (to turn a raw pork belly into bacon) or applying a "secondary rub" to enhance the flavor of store-bought or already-cured bacon.

### Primary Dry Cure Rub (For 5 lbs of Pork Belly)
This is a foundational cure used to transform raw pork belly into bacon over 7–10 days.
*   **Base:** 1/4 cup Kosher salt and 1/4 cup Brown sugar.
*   **Essential:** 1–2 tsp Pink Curing Salt (Prague Powder #1). This is critical for safety and the classic pink color.
*   **Flavoring:** 2 tbsp Cracked black pepper, 1 tsp Paprika, and 1 tsp Red pepper flakes.
*   **Method:** Rub the mixture all over the belly, seal it in a bag, and refrigerate for about a week, flipping daily.

### Flavor-Focused Bacon Rubs
If you are looking to season bacon before smoking or baking it, these blends add a massive flavor profile:

#### 1. Maple & Cracked Pepper (Sweet & Savory)
This is a classic "breakfast style" rub that works perfectly for smoking a cured slab.
*

**Dry Mix:** 1/3 cup Brown sugar, 2 tbsp Coarsely cracked black pepper, and 1 tsp Smoked paprika.
*   **Instructions:** After rinsing the cure off the belly, brush it with a thin layer of maple syrup, then dust with the dry rub before putting it in the smoker at 200°F until the internal temp reaches 150°F.

#### 2. Honey Heat (Spicy & Glazed)
Inspired by Michael Symon’s method, this adds a bold, kicky depth to the meat.
*   **Ingredients:** 1/4 cup Honey (as a binder), 1/4 cup Dark brown sugar, 2 tbsp Red pepper flakes, 2 tbsp Smoked paprika, and 1 tsp Cumin.
*   **Instructions:** Coat the meat in the honey first, then apply the dry spice mixture evenly over all sides.

#### 3. Savory "Pork Rub" Style
If you prefer a more commercial "BBQ" flavor profile, you can use a standard pork rub.
*   **Ingredients:** Brown sugar, Garlic powder, Onion powder, Chili powder, and a touch of Cayenne.
*   **Tip:** Some pitmasters recommend using a pre-made award-winning pork rub to save time while ensuring a balanced

sweet-to-heat ratio.

### Pro Cooking Tips
*   **The Pellicle:** After curing and rinsing, let the bacon sit uncovered in the fridge for 12–24 hours. According to [Hey Grill, Hey](https://heygrillhey.com/homemade-smoked-bacon/), this creates a "tacky" surface called a pellicle that helps the smoke and rub stick much better.
*   **Slicing:** Always chill your bacon thoroughly in the freezer for 30–60 minutes before slicing; it makes it much firmer and easier to get thin, even strips.
*   **Rinsing:** If you find your homemade bacon too salty, [The Daring Gourmet](https://www.daringgourmet.com/how-to-make-homemade-bacon/) suggests thoroughly rinsing the belly after its 7-day cure to remove excess surface salt before smoking.
