### 👤 User

In the state of Texas do you have to hand you drivers license to police or can you just show them?

---

### 🤖 Assistant

In Texas, if you are lawfully detained by a peace officer for an alleged violation of a law while operating a motor vehicle, you are required to provide or display your driver's license upon the officer's request. Additionally, you must give your name, driver's license number, residence address, and date of birth if requested by the officer [0, 2, 3].

Refusing to identify yourself can lead to a Class C misdemeanor, punishable by up to a $500 fine. If you provide false information, it can be a Class B misdemeanor, punishable by up to 6 months in county jail and a $2000 fine [0].

### 👤 User

The question is, do I have to physically hand it to them? Or is it sufficient to just show it to them that they can see the information?

---

### 🤖 Assistant

Short answer: You should be prepared to hand it to them.

Why: Texas law requires a driver who is lawfully stopped to “display” their driver’s license on an officer’s request. In practice across Texas, “display” is treated as physically presenting the license so the officer can inspect it (e.g., verify security features, check expiration, run the number). Simply holding it up at the window often won’t be accepted.

Relevant law and guidance:
- Texas Penal Code §38.02(b-1) requires drivers who are lawfully detained to provide or display their driver’s license and, on request, provide name, DL number, address, and DOB. Refusing can be charged as Failure to Identify, typically a Class C misdemeanor; giving false info is more serious. See discussion and 2023 update adding subsection (b-1) [Saputo article summarizing §38.02](https://saputo.law/criminal-law/texas/failure-to-identify/).
- The Texas State Law Library notes drivers must provide their license upon request during a stop and cites Transportation Code §

521.025 and Penal Code §38.02 as the basis for ID requirements when driving [Texas State Law Library guide](https://guides.sll.texas.gov/protest-rights/police).

Practical tip:
- Handing over the physical card is standard and avoids escalation. If you’re concerned about the officer taking the card out of view, you can calmly state you’re handing it over for inspection and would like it returned promptly after they verify it.

### 👤 User

Do passengers have to show an ID?

---

### 🤖 Assistant

Generally, no—passengers in Texas don’t have to show ID just for being in the car.

- If a passenger is lawfully arrested, they must identify themselves (name, address, DOB) under Penal Code §38.02(a).
- If a passenger is lawfully detained (for example, the officer develops reasonable suspicion the passenger committed a separate offense), it’s a crime to give false identifying information, but mere refusal to identify (without arrest) is not an offense for passengers. The 2023 “driver must display ID” rule applies to the operator, not passengers.
- Special cases: If a passenger is carrying a handgun under a License to Carry, they must display their LTC and ID upon request. Also, if the officer has independent legal cause to demand ID (e.g., witness to a crime with “good cause” under §38.02(b)(3)), different rules can apply.

Sources:
- Texas Penal Code §38.02 (Failure to Identify), including 2023 additions applying to drivers, summarized here: [Saputo law guide](http

s://saputo.law/criminal-law/texas/failure-to-identify/).
- Texas State Law Library overview of ID requirements for drivers and specific circumstances: [Texas State Law Library guide](https://guides.sll.texas.gov/protest-rights/police).
