Based on Texas law, **you may be able to have the vehicle towed, but only under specific conditions**. Here's what you need to know:

### **Key Requirements Under Texas Law**

According to [Texas Property Code § 92.0131](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/property-code/prop-sect-92-0131/), landlords must follow strict rules before towing a tenant's vehicle:

1. **Parking rules must be in the lease** — Your parking policies must have been provided to the tenant in writing before or when the lease was signed, and the tenant must have acknowledged them.

2. **Clear signage is required** — If you're enforcing parking rules, proper tow-away signage must be posted according to [Texas Occupations Code § 2308](https://www.texastowinglawyer.com/blog/understanding-texas-occupations-code-section-2308253-unattended-vehicles-in-apartment-complex-parking-facilities).

3. **Immediate towing is allowed only in specific cases** — You can tow immediately (without additional notice) if the vehicle:
   - Blocks a traffic aisle,

entry, or exit
   - Prevents another vehicle from exiting
   - Is in a fire lane or restricted area clearly marked as a tow-away zone
   - Obstructs gates, garbage areas, or emergency access

4. **10-day written notice required for other violations** — For issues like expired registration or parking in the wrong spot (but not blocking anything), you must provide **10 days' written notice** before towing.

### **What You Should Do**

- **Review your lease** — Does it clearly state where tenants can and cannot park?
- **Check your signage** — Are there visible "No Parking" or "Tow Away Zone" signs where the tenant is parking?
- **Document the violation** — Take photos showing where the vehicle is parked and any relevant signage
- **Provide written notice** — If the violation doesn't qualify for immediate towing, send a formal 10-day notice via certified mail

### **Risks of Improper Towing**

If you tow without following proper procedures, you could be liable for:
- A **$100 civil penalty**
- All towing and

storage costs the tenant incurs
- The tenant's attorney fees if they sue and win

### **Bottom Line**

You likely **can** have the vehicle towed, but you must follow Texas law precisely. If the vehicle is blocking access or in a clearly marked tow-away zone, you may tow immediately. Otherwise, provide written notice first. Consider consulting a local attorney to ensure you're complying with all requirements before taking action.
