### 👤 User

Batter that really sticks to fish fillets

---

### 🤖 Assistant

To get a batter that truly sticks to fish fillets, the secret lies more in the **preparation of the fish** than the recipe itself. If the fish is wet, the batter will steam from the inside out, causing the coating to slide off in one piece.

### The "Golden Rules" for Adhesion
*   **Bone-Dry Fish:** This is the most important step. Pat the fillets with paper towels until they are completely dry to the touch.
*   **The Flour "Primer":** Before dipping the fish into the wet batter, dredge it lightly in plain flour or cornstarch. This acts as a "glue" that helps the wet batter grip the surface of the fish.
*   **Cold Batter, Hot Oil:** Use ice-cold liquids (water, milk, or beer) for the batter and ensure your oil is at least $365^{\circ}F$ ($185^{\circ}C$). If the oil is too cool, the batter will soak up grease and fall off.

### Recommended Batter Recipes

#### 1. The "Unbelievable" Crispy Batter
This is a classic, puffy, pub-style batter that is highly rated for its ability to stay attached to the fish.
*

**Ingredients:** 1 cup all-purpose flour, 1/2 cup milk, 1/2 cup cold water (or club soda), 2 tbsp baking powder, and 1 tsp salt.
*   **Why it works:** The high amount of baking powder creates a light, airy structure that "hugs" the fillet as it expands.
*   **Source:** [Allrecipes](https://www.allrecipes.com/recipe/145853/unbelievable-fish-batter/)

#### 2. The Cornstarch "Shatter" Batter
For a thinner, crunchier coating that mimics Long John Silver's style, use a mix of flour and cornstarch.
*   **Ingredients:** 1/2 cup flour, 1/2 cup cornstarch, 1 tsp baking powder, 1/2 tsp baking soda, and 1 cup of club soda or light beer.
*   **Why it works:** Cornstarch prevents gluten development, resulting in a "shatteringly" crisp crust that doesn't get doughy or peel away.
*   **Source:** [Binky's Culinary Carnival](https://www.binkysculinarycarnival.com/crispiest-fish-batter/)

### Pro-Tips for Success
*   **Don't Overmix:** Whisk the batter until just combined. A few small lumps are fine; overmixing develops

gluten, which makes the batter "rubbery" and more likely to shrink away from the fish.
*   **The "Drip" Test:** After dipping, let the excess batter drip off for a few seconds. A layer that is too thick will be heavy and may pull away from the fish during frying.
*   **Resting:** Once fried, place the fish on a **wire rack** rather than paper towels. Air circulation prevents steam from building up under the fish, which is the primary cause of soggy, peeling batter. [Bless This Mess](https://www.blessthismessplease.com/battered-fried-fish/)
