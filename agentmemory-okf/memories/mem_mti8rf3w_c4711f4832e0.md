### Operation Credible (1979–1980): what it was

**Operation Credible (also called “Credible Sport”)** was a **U.S. military plan in 1980** to **rescue the American hostages held in Iran** during the Iran hostage crisis.

#### What it involved
- It was a **follow-on** to the failed April 1980 rescue attempt (**Operation Eagle Claw**).
- The concept was to use **specially modified aircraft**—most famously a **C-130 Hercules fitted with rockets**—to land in a **very tight space inside/near Tehran (a stadium concept was central)**, rapidly unload forces, and extract hostages.

#### When it happened
- Planning and development ran mainly in **1980** (late spring through fall).
- It was **cancelled in late 1980** after several serious **accidents during testing/training** and as political conditions changed (the hostages were ultimately released in January 1981 through diplomacy, not a rescue).

#### Key takeaway
If you’re seeing it referenced in “1979 or 1980,” that’s because:
- The **hostage crisis began in

1979** (hostages taken Nov 1979),
- But **Operation Credible/Credible Sport itself was a 1980 rescue plan**.

### 🤖 Assistant

You’re thinking of **Operation Eagle Claw** (also called **Operation Desert One**) in **April 1980**—the **first** U.S. attempt to rescue the Iran hostages.

### What happened in the dust storm and collision (casualties)
- On the way into Iran, the force hit **severe dust storms** (“haboobs”), which caused **helicopter problems** and delays.
- At the staging site in Iran (“**Desert One**”), during repositioning for refueling, a **U.S. RH-53D helicopter** collided with a **U.S. C-130**.
- The collision caused a **fire/explosion**.

**Casualties from that accident: 8 U.S. servicemen killed** (and additional personnel injured).

### Clarifying the aircraft detail
It wasn’t a “C-130 colliding in a dust storm” mid-flight; the fatal collision occurred **on the ground at Desert One** after the mission had been aborted, with dust/poor visibility contributing to the mishap.
