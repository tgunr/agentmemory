### 👤 User

What is meritocracy

---

### 🤖 Assistant

Meritocracy is a social system, society, or organization where people get power, roles, and responsibilities based on their individual abilities and achievements rather than their social status, wealth, or family connections. In a meritocratic system, advancement is determined by performance, typically measured through examinations or demonstrated proficiency.

The term was actually coined by British sociologist Michael Young in his 1958 satirical book *The Rise of the Meritocracy*. He used it to describe a future society where social position was determined by the formula:

$$I + E = M$$

In this equation, $I$ represents intelligence, $E$ represents effort, and $M$ represents merit. While Young intended the term to be a warning about a new kind of social stratification, it has since been adopted as a positive ideal in many modern societies.

Proponents of meritocracy argue that it is the most efficient and fair way to organize a society because it ensures that the most capable people are in the most

important positions. It is often associated with the idea of "equality of opportunity," where everyone starts at the same line and their success depends entirely on their own hard work and talent.

However, critics point out several challenges with the concept:

*   It assumes a "level playing field" that rarely exists, as factors like early childhood education, nutrition, and social networks provide some people with significant head starts.
*   It can lead to a "winner-take-all" mentality that ignores the role of luck or systemic advantages.
*   It may create a new elite class that believes their success is entirely self-made, potentially leading to a lack of empathy for those who have not achieved the same status.

In practice, most modern institutions—such as universities and corporations—strive to be meritocratic by using standardized testing, performance reviews, and competitive hiring processes to identify and reward talent.
