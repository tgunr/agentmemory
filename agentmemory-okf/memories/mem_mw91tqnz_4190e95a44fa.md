The user is asking for legal/tax advice about working with their son's small business while on VA disability (100% P&T unemployable / TDIU) without increasing income. This is a substantive question involving tax law, VA rules, and business structure.

Key issues:
1. TDIU: "100% unemployable" — this is likely TDIU (Total Disability Individual Unemployability) under 38 CFR § 4.16. Key point: TDIU beneficiaries CAN work, but earnings above the poverty threshold (currently ~$15,300/yr for 2025) jeopardize benefits. VA doesn't prohibit all work — it's about "substantially gainful employment." Marginal/sheltered employment can be OK.
2. SGA for SSA — if drawing SSDI, SGA limits apply (~$1,620/mo 2025 for non-blind). But user says "SSA" — could be retirement (FRA), in which case earnings test may not apply. Need to clarify.
3. Tax: If unpaid, no income to them. But "occasional cash" — how to get cash without tax liability.
4. Gifts: son can gift up to $19,000/yr (2025) per person with no gift tax, no income tax to recipient. Gifts are not taxable income to the recipient. That's the cleanest route.
5. Business expenses: Provide things to sell — if he sells them, son keeps proceeds; father is a hobbyist? Father's materials costs could be reimbursed (not income).
6. Reimbursement for expenses is not income (accountable plan). So materials/consumables reimbursed at cost = no income.
7. If he gets a W-2, that's earned income — SGA problem and tax.
8. Payments as a contractor/1099 = self-employment income, potential SGA issue, SE tax.
9. Gift versus compensation: IRS cares whether it's really payment for services (payroll tax dodge). But a true gift from a son with no obligation is fine. Note: son's business can't deduct gifts over $25/business gifts rule (that applies to gifts to customers, actually §274(b) limits deduction for business gifts to $25/recipient/yr). Gifts to an employee... if the son gifts money personally (not from business), it's clean. If the business pays, and the father provides services, IRS may call it wages.
10. Better structure: father as a member of an LLC? Partnership income flows through — that'd be income to the father. Not good.
11. Hobby rules: If father's activity is a hobby, expenses may not be deductible post-TCJA (misc itemized deduction suspended through 2025). But if he's not selling himself, no issue.
12. VA rules detail: 38 CFR 4.16(a) TDIU requires inability to secure/maintain substantially gainful employment. VA regulation: "substantially gainful employment" = work that provides annual income exceeding the poverty threshold for one person. Under 38 CFR 4.18, VA considers marginal employment.
