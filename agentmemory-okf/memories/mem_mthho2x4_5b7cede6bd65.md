### 🤖 Assistant — 2026-08-18T22:53:55Z

**Around 350–375°F (177–191°C) for the frying oil** is the key temperature that matters for hush puppy dough/batter. The batter itself is typically mixed and used at room temperature (or lightly chilled), not held to a precise dough temperature like yeast doughs.

### Frying Oil Temperature
- Most reliable recipes target **365°F** as a sweet spot, or a range of **350–375°F**.
- Start the oil at the higher end (around 365–375°F). It will drop when you add the batter; keep it from falling below ~350°F.
- Too low → greasy, dense, or falling-apart puppies that absorb oil.  
- Too high → dark/burnt outside with undercooked, gummy centers.

Use a deep-fry or candy thermometer and fry in small batches so the temperature recovers between additions. Cast-iron pots help hold heat steadily.<grok:render card_id="19a81d" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">0</argument></grok:render>

### Batter/Dough Notes
Hush puppy “dough” is a thick, drop-style batter (cornmeal + flour + liquid + usually egg/onion). It is usually mixed just until combined (do not overmix) and can be:
- Used right away at room temperature, or
- Rested 10–15 minutes (or refrigerated 30 minutes to overnight) so it firms up and holds its shape better when dropped into hot oil. Chilled batter is less likely to spread or fall apart.<grok:render card_id="deeae7" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">11</argument></grok:render>

Form into roughly tablespoon- to golf-ball-sized portions.

### Doneness
Cook until deep golden brown (usually 2–4 minutes, turning as needed). For food safety with the flour/cornmeal, the internal temperature should reach at least **165°F** (74°C). Wetter batters may benefit from going closer to 200°F for better texture.<grok:render card_id="b03833" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">0</argument></grok:render>

Drain on paper towels and serve hot. Temperature control of the oil is far more important than the exact temperature of the batter itself.
