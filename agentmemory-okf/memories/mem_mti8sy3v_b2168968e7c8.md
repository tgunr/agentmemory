### 🤖 Assistant

A classic roast chicken is one of the most rewarding dishes you can master, relying on just a few key techniques to achieve shatteringly crisp skin and succulent, tender meat. The secret to a perfect bird often lies in the preparation before it even hits the oven, such as letting the chicken sit uncovered in the refrigerator for a few hours to dry out the skin or generously seasoning it with kosher salt well in advance.

To get started, preheat your oven to 425°F (218°C). Pat the chicken completely dry with paper towels, as any moisture on the surface will steam the meat rather than crisp the skin. Rub the entire bird with softened butter or olive oil, then season aggressively with salt and freshly cracked black pepper. For extra aromatics, you can stuff the cavity with a halved lemon, a head of garlic cut in cross-sections, and a bundle of fresh herbs like thyme, rosemary, or sage.

Place the chicken in a roasting pan or a large cast-iron skillet, which helps conduct heat evenly to the bottom of the bird.

Roast for about 50 to 60 minutes, or until a meat thermometer inserted into the thickest part of the thigh reaches 165°F (74°C). One of the most critical steps is to let the chicken rest for at least 15 minutes after taking it out of the oven; this allows the juices to redistribute throughout the meat so they don't run out the moment you carve it.

### 🤖 Assistant

Slow roasting a chicken at a lower temperature transforms the meat into something incredibly tender and succulent, almost like a confit, while allowing the flavors of any aromatics to deeply penetrate the bird. This method is much more forgiving than high-heat roasting and results in meat that practically falls off the bone.

To begin, preheat your oven to a low temperature, typically around 300°F (150°C). Because you aren't relying on high heat to crisp the skin quickly, it is even more important to season the chicken well in advance, ideally the night before, with plenty of kosher salt. This dry-brining process seasons the meat deeply and helps the skin eventually brown even at lower temperatures.

Before placing the chicken in the oven, rub it generously with olive oil or softened butter mixed with herbs like thyme, rosemary, and perhaps a bit of smoked paprika for color. Place the bird in a heavy-duty roasting pan or a Dutch oven. You can surround the chicken with hearty root vegetables like carrots,

parsnips, and shallots, which will slowly caramelize in the chicken fat over the long cooking period.

Roast the chicken for about 2.5 to 3 hours, depending on its size. You are looking for an internal temperature of 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part of the thigh, but with slow roasting, the meat remains moist even if it goes slightly over. If the skin isn't as brown or crisp as you'd like at the end, you can turn the oven up to 450°F (230°C) for the last 10 to 15 minutes to get that golden finish.

As with any roast, let the chicken rest for at least 20 minutes before carving. The result is a deeply flavorful, melt-in-your-mouth texture that is quite different from the snappy, firm texture of a high-heat roast.
