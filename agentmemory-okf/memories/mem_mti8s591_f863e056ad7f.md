### 👤 User

How much epoxy do I need to flood coat a 9x11 board and sides that are half inch high?

---

### 🤖 Assistant

To flood coat a 9x11 board with half-inch sides, you will need approximately 8.5 to 9 ounces of epoxy.

This calculation is based on a standard flood coat thickness of 1/8 inch. The total surface area includes the 99 square inches for the top and 20 square inches for the four sides, totaling 119 square inches. At a 1/8-inch thickness, this requires about 14.88 cubic inches of epoxy, which converts to roughly 8.25 fluid ounces.

It is generally recommended to mix a small amount extra (around 10%) to account for waste, drips, and ensuring full coverage, bringing the total to about 9 ounces.

### 👤 User

I have a plaque that I'm making from a white oak board and a UV sticker. On top of the sticker I want a clear coat epoxy layer. Should I seal the board before I put on the sticker or after?

---

### 🤖 Assistant

You should **seal the board before** you put on the sticker.

Here's why:

**Seal the wood first** with a thin coat of epoxy. This prevents:
- Air bubbles from escaping through the porous white oak grain
- The wood from absorbing moisture from the epoxy later
- Uneven adhesion

**Then apply your UV sticker** to the sealed, cured surface. Make sure to burnish it well to eliminate air pockets.

**Finally, apply your clear epoxy flood coat** over the sticker.

This "sandwich method" is the recommended approach for embedding stickers and decals in epoxy. According to woodworking forums, you should [seal the surface with epoxy, sand to roughen for the next bond layer, apply stickers, then cast thick epoxy over the top](https://www.lumberjocks.com/threads/table-with-vinyl-decals-under-epoxy.306095/).

The initial seal coat also gives the sticker a better surface to adhere to and helps prevent the epoxy from seeping under the edges of the sticker.

### 👤 User

Can I use a UV epoxy for the ceiling layer and then put the sticker and then the clear coat epoxy?

---

### 🤖 Assistant

Yes, you can use UV epoxy for the sealing layer! That's actually a smart approach because:

**Benefits of UV epoxy as the seal coat:**
- **Cures in minutes** instead of overnight, so you can move to the next step faster
- Creates a smooth, sealed surface for the sticker
- Prevents air bubbles from the wood grain
- No waiting around for 24 hours between steps

**Your workflow would be:**
1. Apply thin UV epoxy coat to the white oak board
2. Cure it with a UV light (usually 2-5 minutes depending on thickness)
3. Lightly sand with fine grit (220-320) to give the sticker something to grip
4. Apply your UV sticker and burnish well
5. Apply your clear coat epoxy (regular 2-part epoxy) over the top

**One tip:** Make sure the UV epoxy and your final clear coat epoxy are compatible. Most are, but it's worth doing a small test piece first to ensure they bond well together. Some UV epoxies can remain slightly tacky on the surface, which actually helps with adhesion to the next layer.

This method lets you complete the

project much faster than waiting for a traditional epoxy seal coat to cure!
