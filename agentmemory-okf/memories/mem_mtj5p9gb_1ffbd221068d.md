---

### 🤖 Assistant

Sounds like that young buck’s gut is in a bad way, and you’re right — watery scours in a goat often points to a heavy worm load, though it can also be coccidiosis or even diet-related.  
Worms are a real quick killer in goats if they get ahead of you, so here’s how I’d tackle it — fast, but smart.  

---

**1. Get him separated right now**  
- Move him into a clean, dry pen away from the rest of the herd.  
- Keep fresh water handy, and give him good-quality hay only — no grain for now.  

---

**2. Check for dehydration**  
- Pinch the skin on his neck — if it stays “tented” instead of snapping back, he’s dry.  
- If so, get fluids into him — electrolytes (like ReSorb or homemade with salt, baking soda, and molasses) help.  
- Severe dehydration may need subcutaneous or IV fluids from a vet.  

---

**3. Treat for worms — but pick the right product**  
The fastest knockdown for gut worms in goats is usually one of these:  

| Drug (Brand) | Active Ingredient | Dosage for Goats* | Notes |
|--------------|------------------|------------------|-------|
| **Valbazen** | albendazole | 1 ml per 22 lb orally | Avoid in pregnant does. Kills tapes + strongyles. |
| **Prohibit** | levamisole | 3 ml per 50 lb orally | Very effective but easy to overdose — weigh goat! |
| **Safeguard** | fenbendazole | 1 ml per 5 lb orally | Needs higher/longer dose for goats; resistance common. |
| **Ivermectin Plus** | ivermectin + clorsulon | 1 ml per 33 lb orally | Kills strongyles + liver flukes. Resistance in some areas. |

\*Goat doses are usually 2× the sheep label — always weigh the goat to dose right.  

- Give the drench orally with a syringe, aiming over the tongue into the back of the mouth so it swallows.  
- In bad cases, I’ll repeat in 10 days to break the life cycle.  

---

**4. Don’t forget coccidia**  
- Watery, sometimes bloody diarrhea in a young goat can be coccidiosis — worms and coccidia can tag-team.  
- If you suspect it (or if the wormer doesn’t help in 24–48 hrs), treat with **Corid** (amprolium) or **Baycox** (toltrazuril).  

---

**5. Support while he heals**  
- Electrolytes daily until stools firm up.  
- A little plain yogurt or probiotic paste to restore gut flora.  
- Keep him warm and stress-free.  

---

**6. Vet and fecal check**  
- If he’s not brighter within 48 hours, or if you’re unsure of the parasite, a quick fecal test will tell you exactly what you’re fighting — that’s the best way to avoid resistance and pick the right drug.
