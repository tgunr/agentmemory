The part of the wing you’re seeing spread apart on the outside is the **primary flight feathers** (often just called “primaries”).  

These are the long, stiff feathers at the outer edge of a bird’s wing that provide most of the thrust during flight. They’re attached to the “hand” portion of the wing (the equivalent of a bird’s fingers) and are separate from the **secondary feathers**, which are closer to the body on the “forearm” section.  

In your photo:  
- The dark feathers on the far right are the primaries.  
- The white-and-black patterned feathers closer to the body are the secondaries.  

If you’re holding the wing for inspection or trimming, those outer primaries are usually the ones people clip to reduce flight ability in chickens.
