### 👤 User

I have an idea for my maple plaque which uses a full color eagle and the USPIS federal agent badge. I want to create a raised texture of each and then apply it to the plaque like a DTF transfer. Obviously I can't use DTF with a raised texture so I need something to print on that will bind like DTF to the maple.

---

### 🤖 Assistant

That's an interesting concept, but the challenge is that DTF's strength comes from its hot-melt adhesive layer, not just the film itself. Once you add a significant raised texture, you move outside what DTF film is designed to do.

A few approaches are much more likely to work:

- UV DTF with varnish (my top recommendation)
  - Print the artwork on UV DTF film.
  - Build the relief using multiple layers of clear UV varnish during printing.
  - Transfer it to the maple like a standard UV DTF decal.
  - This gives a tactile raised effect (typically 0.2-0.8 mm depending on the printer).
  - Very durable and no heat is required.

- Print on a thin printable polymer film
  - Materials such as 5-10 mil clear polyester (PET), polycarbonate (Lexan), or white PVC can be UV printed with many layers to create texture.
  - Laser cut the outline.
  - Bond to the maple using a permanent optically clear adhesive (3M 467MP or 468MP transfer adhesive are excellent choices).
  - Because these adhesives are only about 0.05-0.

13 mm thick, the piece looks almost integrated into the wood.

- Print on printable polyurethane (TPU)
  - TPU remains flexible like DTF.
  - UV print the color and raised varnish.
  - Laminate a heat-activated adhesive to the back if you want a heat-applied transfer.
  - This is closer to a true "raised DTF" but requires more process development.

- Cast clear resin doming
  - Print the graphics onto thin clear film.
  - Apply a controlled UV resin dome over the eagle and badge.
  - Bond the finished piece to the plaque.
  - Creates a premium, dimensional appearance.

For maple plaques specifically, I would avoid trying to heat-press anything with thick texture. Maple is rigid, and thicker films don't conform well under heat. Pressure-sensitive acrylic adhesives generally produce a cleaner result.

One thing I should mention is that the USPIS Federal Agent badge is an official government insignia. Reproducing it on a product may involve legal or policy restrictions depending on how it is will be used,

so it's worth confirming you have permission or that your intended use is allowed.

If you're already doing UV printing, here's what I would build:

- Material: 0.010" clear polycarbonate (Lexan) or 8-10 mil PET.
- Front print:
  - White underbase.
  - CMYK artwork.
  - 10-30 layers of clear varnish for the feather and badge details.
- Back:
  - Apply 3M 467MP transfer adhesive.
  - Laser cut to the contour.
  - Peel liner and apply to the finished maple plaque.

The finished part is extremely thin around the edges, but the eagle and badge themselves have noticeable relief, giving the appearance that they were directly printed onto the wood.

Can you tell me:
- What UV printer are you using (Mimaki, Roland, Epson, etc.)?
- Approximately how much raised texture do you want—around 0.010" (0.25 mm), 0.030" (0.75 mm), or closer to 0.060" (1.5 mm)?
- Are you looking for a glossy domed look or a textured, sculpted surface that resembles carved wood?
