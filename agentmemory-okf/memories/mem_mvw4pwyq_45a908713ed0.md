**Long COVID** (technical name: post-acute sequelae of SARS-CoV-2, or PASC) is a chronic condition where symptoms persist or newly appear weeks to months after the initial COVID-19 infection clears.

**How it's defined:** generally symptoms lasting **3+ months** after infection, not explained by another diagnosis. It can follow mild, moderate, or severe initial illness — even asymptomatic cases.

**Common symptoms:**
- **Fatigue** and post-exertional malaise (symptoms worsen after physical or mental effort) — the hallmark
- Brain fog, memory/concentration problems, headaches
- Shortness of breath, palpitations, chest pain
- Loss/distortion of smell or taste
- Sleep disturbance, anxiety, depression
- GI issues, joint/muscle pain, rashes

**What's known about biology:** proposed mechanisms include viral persistence in tissue reservoirs, immune dysregulation/autoimmunity, microclotting and endothelial damage, reactivation of latent viruses (e.g. EBV), and mitochondrial dysfunction. No single mechanism is confirmed, and there's no diagnostic test or approved treatment — care is symptom-directed (pacing, rehab, treating specific organ involvement).

**Scale:** estimates vary widely — roughly 10–30% of infected people report persistent symptoms in some cohorts, less in others; risk appears lower after vaccination and with newer variants, but isn't zero. It's recognized as a disabling condition by WHO and is a leading cause of post-pandemic workforce loss.

Persistent throat phlegm after COVID is very common and usually has a mundane explanation — but it's worth knowing which type you have, because the treatments differ.

**Most likely causes (roughly in order of frequency)**

1. **Post-nasal drip / chronic rhinitis** — the most common. Post-COVID sinus and nasal inflammation keeps producing mucus that runs down the back of the throat. You feel it as "phlegm" but it's coming from above. Often worse lying down, mornings.
2. **Globus pharyngeus** — a sensation of a lump or mucus in the throat with little or nothing actually there. Linked to throat muscle tension, reflux, and anxiety. Very common after illness and notoriously persistent; the sensation is real, the mucus isn't.
3. **LPR (laryngopharyngeal reflux)** — "silent reflux." Stomach contents reach the larynx, causing throat clearing, mucus feeling, hoarseness, worse in the morning. Notably, LPR often has **no heartburn**. COVID and post-viral cough can worsen it.
4. **Airway inflammation / post-viral cough hypersensitivity** — irritated vagal nerve endings make you clear your throat constantly, which itself makes more mucus and irritation (a self-perpetuating loop).
