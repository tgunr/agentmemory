### 👤 User

What dishes are cooked with a coding coating of salt on both sides?

---

### 🤖 Assistant

Dishes that are cooked with a coating of salt on both sides primarily fall into two categories: those using a "salt crust" to entomb the food and those where a heavy initial seasoning acts as a functional step in the cooking process.

### Salt-Crusted Dishes
The most prominent "coating" method is salt-crusting, where a thick layer of moistened salt (sometimes mixed with egg whites) is packed onto all sides of the food to create a sealed chamber.

*   **Salt-Baked Whole Fish:** This is the most classic example. A whole fish (such as sea bass or snapper) is buried in a thick mound of kosher salt on both sides. When baked, the salt hardens into a shell that traps steam, seasoning the fish deeply while keeping the flesh incredibly succulent. It is recommended to pull the fish at $130^{\circ}F$ as the salt crust retains significant heat and causes more dramatic carryover cooking than open roasting [Serious Eats](https://www.seriouseats.com/salt-baked-whole-fish-with-fresh-herbs-5212108).
*   **

Salt-Crusted Poultry or Meat:** Similar to fish, whole chickens or large roasts (like beef tenderloin) can be encased in salt to produce an even, moist cook. The "coating" here is often several inches thick and is cracked open like a shell before serving.

### Heavy Surface Coating for Texture and Prep
In other dishes, a coating of salt is applied to both sides not to create a shell, but to transform the surface of the ingredient.

*   **Steaks and Chops:** For a restaurant-quality crust, chefs recommend salting both sides "liberally" or "generously" before cooking. This creates an even, visible layer that resembles a "dusting of snow." This salt coating helps the exterior carry the flavor weight for the unseasoned interior and aids in the Maillard reaction for a better sear [Serious Eats](https://www.seriouseats.com/how-to-salt-meat-117

97356).
*   **Salt-Drained Vegetables:** Vegetables with high water content, such as **eggplant (aubergine)**, **zucchini**, and **tomatoes**, are often coated with salt on both sides before cooking. This "coating" draws out excess moisture, which is then patted dry. This prevents the vegetables from becoming soggy during frying or roasting and is often repeated on both sides for larger slices [Jungle Jim's](https://junglejims.com/everything-you-need-to-know-about-salting-your-food/).
*   **Salt Block Cooking:** When using a Himalayan salt block, the block itself acts as the cooking surface. For items like halibut or salmon, the fish is often seasoned on both sides and placed directly onto the super-heated salt surface, which continues to "coat" the food with mineral-rich salt as it sears [Salts Worldwide](https://saltsworldwide.com/cooking-on-a-salt-block-a-guide-for-beginners/).
