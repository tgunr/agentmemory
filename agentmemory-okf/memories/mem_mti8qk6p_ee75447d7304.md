Pickling eggs

### 🤖 Assistant

Pickling eggs is simple and honestly kind of addictive once you start. Here’s a solid, classic way to do it:

### What you need
- 6–12 hard-boiled eggs (peeled)
- 1 cup white vinegar
- 1 cup water
- 1 tbsp sugar (optional, for slight sweetness)
- 1–2 tsp salt
- Spices (your choice):  
  - peppercorns  
  - garlic cloves  
  - bay leaf  
  - mustard seeds  
  - chili flakes (if you want heat)  
  - sliced onion or beet (beets give that pink color)

### Steps
1. Hard-boil your eggs, cool them, and peel.
2. In a saucepan, combine vinegar, water, salt, sugar, and spices. Bring to a boil, then simmer a couple minutes.
3. Pack eggs into a clean jar.
4. Pour the hot pickling liquid over the eggs until fully covered.
5. Let cool, then seal and refrigerate.

### How long to wait
- Minimum: 24 hours  
- Better: 3–5 days  
- Best flavor: about 1–2 weeks

### Tips
- Keep them refrigerated (these aren’t shelf-stable like canned foods).
- They last ~3–4 weeks in the fridge.
- The longer they sit, the stronger the

flavor—and the yolks eventually get pickled too.

If you want, I can give you a spicy bar-style version or the classic beet pickled eggs.

Beet version

### 🤖 Assistant

The beet-pickled version is a classic—it turns the egg whites a vibrant, deep magenta and adds a subtle earthy sweetness that pairs perfectly with the tangy vinegar.

### What you need
- 6–12 hard-boiled eggs (peeled)
- 1 can (15 oz) of sliced beets (keep the liquid!) OR 2 cups of fresh cooked beet juice
- 1 cup white vinegar or apple cider vinegar
- 1/4 to 1/2 cup sugar (adjust based on how sweet you like them)
- 1 tsp salt
- 1/2 sliced onion (optional, but highly recommended)
- Your spices: 1 cinnamon stick, a few cloves, and some peppercorns (this gives that traditional "spiced" beet flavor)

### How to make them
1. Pack your peeled eggs, the sliced beets from the can, and the sliced onion into a large glass jar.
2. In a saucepan, mix the vinegar, sugar, salt, and the liquid from the beet can.
3. Bring the mixture to a boil, stirring until the sugar is dissolved.
4. Pour the hot liquid into the jar, making sure the eggs are completely submerged.
5. Seal the jar once cooled and put it in the fridge.

###

Timing and Color
- **24 hours:** You'll get a thin pink ring around the outside of the white.
- **3 days:** The color will penetrate about halfway through the white.
- **7 days:** The color usually reaches the yolk, and the flavor is fully developed.

### A few extra tips
- If you use apple cider vinegar, the flavor will be a bit mellower and fruitier than white vinegar.
- Don't throw away the beet slices in the jar; they pick up the pickling spice and are delicious on salads.
- For a "deviled" twist, wait a week, then use these eggs to make deviled eggs—the purple whites against the yellow filling look incredible.
