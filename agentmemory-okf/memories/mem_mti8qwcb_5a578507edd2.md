### OrcaSlicer vs. PrusaSlicer — pros and cons

#### OrcaSlicer
Pros
- Excellent out-of-the-box experience for many popular printers (esp. Bambu; solid community profiles for Creality, Voron, etc.).
- Built‑in calibration wizards (flow, pressure advance, input shaper/Klipper workflows) make tuning fast.
- Modern, streamlined UI with lots of QoL: seam painting, support painting, per‑object/per‑layer settings, project presets.
- Strong high‑speed printing focus (good defaults for acceleration, jerk, PA; handy speed/quality tradeoff presets).
- Smooth AMS workflows (multi‑color/material) for Bambu users; good multi‑material UX in general.

Cons
- Fast‑moving fork—features change quickly; docs can lag and occasional regressions happen.
- Best experience is with Bambu and Klipper‑style high‑speed setups; profiles for niche printers may vary in quality.
- Ecosystem integrations (device management, LIDAR checks, AMS) are Bambu‑centric.

Best for
- Bambu printer owners.
- Klipper/high‑speed printers where quick

calibration and speed tuning matter.
- Users who want convenient wizards and polished UX.

#### PrusaSlicer
Pros
- Mature, stable, and very powerful feature set; excellent geometric tools (modifiers, per‑feature/per‑object overrides).
- Organic supports, paint‑on supports, variable layer height, seam painting, ironing—battle‑tested.
- Strong documentation and predictable releases; large ecosystem and community.
- First‑class support for Prusa printers and MMU; robust conditional G‑code and advanced scripting.

Cons
- UI/UX can feel denser and more technical; fewer hand‑holding wizards for calibration.
- Defaults are conservative for non‑Prusa printers; third‑party profiles may need tweaking.
- Release cadence is slower; some QoL from newer forks may arrive later.

Best for
- Prusa printer owners (MK3/MK4/XL) and MMU users.
- Power users who want maximal control, modifiers, and scripting.
- Those prioritizing stability and documentation over rapid new features.

#### Feature parity notes
- Both support:

organic/paint‑on supports, variable layer height, seam painting, per‑object settings, multi‑material with wipe towers, ironing, Arachne perimeter engine.
- Differences tend to be in UX polish, calibration tooling, ecosystem integrations, and default profiles.

#### Quick pick
- Use OrcaSlicer if you have a Bambu or a high‑speed/Klipper setup and want fast, guided tuning with a modern UI.
- Use PrusaSlicer if you have a Prusa printer/MMU, need maximum control with rock‑solid stability and documentation.
