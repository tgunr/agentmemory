### 🤖 Assistant

Sounds like you’re talking about **water glass eggs**—an old-school way to preserve fresh eggs for months (even up to a year).

Here’s the quick rundown:

### What it is
“Water glass” = **sodium silicate solution**. It seals the eggshell pores so bacteria and air can’t get in.

### How it works
- You take **fresh, unwashed eggs** (important—natural bloom must be intact)
- Submerge them in a mixture of **water + sodium silicate**
- Store them in a cool place
- The eggs stay usable long-term without refrigeration

### Basic ratio
- 1 part sodium silicate  
- 9–10 parts boiled, cooled water  

### Steps
- Mix solution in a clean container  
- Gently place eggs pointy-end down  
- Make sure they’re fully covered  
- Seal container loosely and store cool  

### How long they last
- Typically **6–12 months**

### When you use them later
- Whites get thinner, so they’re best for **baking or cooking**, not frying pretty eggs  
- Always crack into a separate bowl first (just in case)

### Important notes
- Only use **

very fresh eggs**
- Don’t wash them beforehand
- If an egg floats or smells off → toss it

Limewater eggs

---

### 🤖 Assistant

**Limewater eggs** (aka *lime-pickled eggs*, not the same as pickled-in-vinegar) are a super practical, old-school way to store fresh eggs for **months without refrigeration**.

### What it is
You preserve eggs in a solution of **hydrated lime (calcium hydroxide)** + water. It seals the shell and keeps bacteria out.

### What you need
- Fresh, unwashed eggs (with bloom intact)  
- **Pickling lime** (food-grade calcium hydroxide — *not garden lime*)  
- Clean water  
- Glass jar or food-safe bucket with lid  

### Basic ratio
- 1 ounce (by weight) pickling lime  
- 1 quart water  

(Or roughly 1 cup lime per 1 gallon water)

### Steps
- Mix lime into water (it’ll look cloudy)  
- Gently place eggs in container  
- Pour solution over until fully covered  
- Keep them submerged (you can add more solution anytime)  
- Cover and store in a cool, dark place  

### How long they last
- Commonly **6–12 months**, sometimes longer if stored well  

### Using them later
- Rinse the egg before cracking  
- Crack into a

separate bowl first  
- Best for **scrambling, baking, boiling**  
- Fried eggs work, but whites can be a bit thinner

### Important tips
- Only use **clean-looking but unwashed eggs**  
- No cracks—those go bad fast  
- If it floats or smells off → toss it  
- Keep eggs submerged at all times  

### Why people prefer this over water glass
- Easier to find (pickling lime is common)  
- Less finicky  
- Long track record in homesteading

### 👤 User

Does rinsing eggs out with water count as cleaning them?

---

### 🤖 Assistant

Yes, rinsing eggs with water definitely counts as cleaning them, and for the purpose of preserving eggs (like water glassing or using limewater), **you should not do it.**

Here is why rinsing matters so much for preservation:

#### The "Bloom" (Cuticle)
When a hen lays an egg, it is covered in a natural, invisible protective coating called the "bloom." This coating seals the thousands of tiny pores in the eggshell, preventing oxygen and bacteria from getting inside. 

#### Why rinsing is "cleaning"
The moment water touches the egg—even if it's just a quick rinse without soap—the bloom is dissolved and washed away. Once the bloom is gone:
*   The shell becomes porous.
*   Bacteria can enter the egg more easily.
