### 🤖 Assistant

From the labels in your photo, this appears to be a 2:1 mixing-ratio casting epoxy ("Crystal Clear • 2:1 Mixing Ratio").

You mixed:
- 150 mL from the blue-labeled container
- 300 mL from the red-labeled container

That is a 1:2 ratio, not 2:1.

The key question is: which container is Part A (resin) and which is Part B (hardener)? The labels shown don't reveal that side of the container.

If the product requires:
- 2 parts resin : 1 part hardener, then 150 mL + 300 mL is far too much hardener.
- 1 part resin : 2 parts hardener, then your mix would be correct.

Most deep-pour casting epoxies with a "2:1 mixing ratio" are mixed 2 parts resin to 1 part hardener by volume, so there is a good chance the batch was mixed incorrectly. An incorrect ratio often results in epoxy that remains soft, rubbery, or permanently tacky.

A few other possibilities:
- Temperature is too low. Deep-pour epoxies can cure very slowly below about 70°F (21°C).
- Incomplete mixing.

Scrape-and-stir time is critical.
- Very large or very shallow pours can affect cure behavior depending on the formulation.

To help determine what's happening:
1. Check the containers and tell me which one is labeled "Part A" and which is "Part B."
2. Tell me the room temperature where it is curing.
3. Tell me how thick the pour is.

With that information I can estimate whether the batch can still cure or whether it will need to be removed and remade.

In that case, a few things could explain why it's still tacky after 8 hours:

- Incomplete mixing. Even with the correct ratio, insufficient stirring can leave uncured regions.
- Measuring error. If the volumes were estimated rather than measured accurately, the actual ratio may have been off.
