### 🤖 Assistant

A tsunami is a series of extremely long waves caused by a large and sudden displacement of the ocean, usually the result of an earthquake below or near the ocean floor. Unlike typical wind-driven waves, tsunamis involve the movement of the entire water column from the surface to the seafloor, allowing them to travel across entire ocean basins with minimal energy loss.

### Current Status and Recent Events (March 2026)
As of March 24, 2026, there are no active tsunami warnings, advisories, or watches in effect globally. However, several regions are currently observing **Tsunami Preparedness Week** (March 22–28, 2026), including Alaska and parts of the U.S. West Coast, to commemorate the anniversary of the 1964 Great Alaska Earthquake.

Recent significant activity includes:
*   **March 23, 2026:** A magnitude 6.2 earthquake occurred south of Upolu Island, Samoa. The [U.S. Tsunami Warning System](https://www.tsunami.gov/) evaluated the event and confirmed there was no tsunami threat to Hawaii, American Samoa,

or the U.S. West Coast.
*   **July 29, 2025:** A major magnitude 8.8 earthquake struck offshore the Kamchatka Peninsula, Russia. This event generated a Pacific-wide tsunami with runup heights reaching 17–19 meters in local areas and causing damage as far away as Crescent City, California.
*   **Ongoing Drills:** Communities in Northern California and Alaska are conducting evacuation drills this week. Experts emphasize that for local "near-field" tsunamis, the shaking itself is the warning, as waves can arrive within minutes—often before official alerts can be broadcast.

### Causes and Characteristics
Tsunamis are most commonly triggered by "megathrust" earthquakes at subduction zones, where one tectonic plate is forced under another. Other causes include:
*   **Underwater Landslides:** Often triggered by earthquakes, these can cause localized but devastating waves.
*   **Volcanic Eruptions:** Such as the 2022 Hunga Tonga-Hunga Ha'apai eruption, which created both a traditional tsunami and a "meteo-tsunami"

caused by atmospheric pressure waves.
*   **Meteorite Impacts:** Though extremely rare, these can displace massive volumes of water.

### Warning Signs and Safety
If you are near a coastal area, the following natural signs indicate a tsunami may be imminent:
*   **Strong or Long Shaking:** An earthquake that lasts 20 seconds or more.
*   **Ocean Withdrawal:** The water receding unusually far, exposing the seafloor and fish.
*   **Loud Roar:** A sound similar to a train or a jet engine coming from the ocean.

In the event of these signs or an official warning, the standard safety protocol is to move immediately to higher ground or as far inland as possible. For more detailed information on historical events and real-time monitoring, you can visit the [National Centers for Environmental Information (NCEI)](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/news/staying-one-step-ahead-tsunamis).
