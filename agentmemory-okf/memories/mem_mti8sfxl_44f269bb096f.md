### 👤 User

Given this image to be made into a plaque what would be the minimum size to be able to read 4 feet way the smallest test with 20/20 vision?

---

### 🤖 Assistant

To determine the minimum size for a plaque that remains legible from 4 feet away for someone with 20/20 vision, we use the standard visual acuity benchmark where a character must subtend approximately 5 arcminutes of the visual field to be readable.

At a distance of 4 feet (48 inches), the minimum physical height for a lowercase letter or detail to be legible is approximately 0.07 inches (specifically $$48 \times \tan(5/60^\circ) \approx 0.0698$$ inches).

In your provided image, the smallest text—found in the bulleted list (e.g., "Unwavering Integrity") and the descriptive paragraphs—occupies roughly 1.67% of the total vertical height of the design (approximately 9 pixels out of the 540-pixel total height).

#### Minimum Recommended Dimensions
To ensure that this smallest text meets the 0.07-inch legibility threshold, the plaque would need to be at least:

*   **Height:** 4.2 inches
*   **Width:** 5.6 inches (maintaining the 4:3 aspect ratio of the image)

#### Practical Considerations
While 4.2" x 5.6" is

the mathematical minimum for 20/20 vision in perfect lighting, plaque manufacturers and accessibility standards (like the ADA) often recommend larger sizes for comfortable reading. For a professional appearance and to account for viewers with slightly less than perfect vision or sub-optimal lighting, a size of **8" x 10"** or **9" x 12"** would be much more effective for a wall-mounted display intended to be read from a few feet away.

### 🤖 Assistant

For an **11×14 professional wall plaque**, your instincts are right—**material choice + process combination** will matter more than size at this point, especially since the client wants the **eagle visible but clearly subordinate to the lettering**.

Below is a practical, shop‑floor–oriented breakdown based on durability, readability, and professional appearance.

---

## 1. Best Overall Recommendation (Most Professional Look)

### **Hardwood + CNC Relief Eagle + V‑Carved Lettering (Optional Laser Cleanup)**
**Material**
- **Maple (Hard or Soft)** – *Top choice*
- **Cherry** – Warmer, more ceremonial
- **Walnut** – Premium, but contrast must be managed carefully

**Why**
- CNC relief gives the eagle **depth without visual clutter**
- V‑bit lettering creates **crisp, shadowed text** that reads well at distance
- Hardwood finishes cleanly and ages well in professional environments

**Execution**
- **CNC**:  
  - Eagle: *shallow 3D relief* (≈ 0.06"–0.1" max depth)
  - Lettering: *60° or 90° V‑bit*, slightly

deeper than eagle
- **Finish**:
  - Darkened eagle background (stain or dye)
  - Natural or lightly stained raised surface
  - Clear satin or matte topcoat

✅ **This gives the clearest hierarchy:**  
**Text first → Symbol second → Background last**

---

## 2. Laser Engraving – When It Works (and When It Doesn’t)

### ✅ Best Uses
- Fine text
- Logos
- Subtle background imagery

### ❌ Limitations for This Project
- Laser-only engraving on wood can make the **eagle too visually busy**
- Shallow depth = less separation between background and text
- Large laser fills can look “burned” rather than dimensional

### If You Use Laser:
- Use it **only for**:
  - Background eagle (very light power)
  - Small body text
- Avoid lasering the main headers if possible
