I’ll pull official details and recent coverage on the iThrone toilet so the picture is accurate.**iThrone** is a waterless, sewerless toilet from change:WATER Labs in Cambridge, Massachusetts. Founder Diana Yousef (PhD biochemistry, Cornell; NASA consultant; MIT research affiliate) designed it so a toilet can work where there is no water line, no sewer, and no power.<grok:render card_id="9fac8f" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">30</argument></grok:render>

Human waste is about 95% water. The iThrone does not add water to move that waste. It removes the water that is already in it.<grok:render card_id="94f047" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">35</argument></grok:render>

What you sit on looks like a compact sit-down toilet. Waste drops into a pouch made of a proprietary breathable polymer membrane (the team calls it “shrink wrap for crap”). Water molecules pass through as vapor; solids, pathogens, and odor molecules stay inside. The driver is vapor-pressure difference between warm, wet waste and drier outside air — no heater, pump, or fan required. Volume drops by about 90–95%, so the pouch can go weeks or months between emptying instead of daily.<grok:render card_id="1d00db" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">33</argument></grok:render>

**What it needs / does not need**
- No flush water
- No sewer hookup
- No electricity for the core evaporative process
- Periodic emptying of the remaining dried solids (often cited as monthly; some home pilots ran 2–3 months)
