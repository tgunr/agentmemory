**The closest freely available STL models for a rolling-diaphragm (also called rolling piston, top-hat, or diaphragm piston) setup are from Robert Murray-Smith / ThinkingandTinkering.**

### Main files
- **Diaphragm Piston** (core assembly): [thingiverse.com/thing:6819005](https://www.thingiverse.com/thing:6819005)  
  Includes STLs for the piston, cylinder, clips/top, etc.  
  Video explanation & demo: [youtu.be/-gvplMQbqnA](https://youtu.be/-gvplMQbqnA)

- **Diaphragm Piston Engine** (full engine using the above): [thingiverse.com/thing:6822399](https://www.thingiverse.com/thing:6822399)  
  Related video: [youtu.be/pOPL0KdLXEc](https://youtu.be/pOPL0KdLXEc)

### How it works / what you need
This is a low-friction seal design where a flexible sleeve (the “diaphragm”) rolls rather than slides between the piston and cylinder wall. The printed parts form the rigid housing and piston; the actual rolling membrane is made from a **natural latex glove finger** (powder-free preferred) plus dry graphite lubricant so it doesn’t stick to itself.

Print settings suggested by the designer (PLA, Bambu X1C example): 0.2 mm layers, 25% infill, supports + rafts, cyanoacrylate glue for assembly. Optional silicone sealant for better airtightness.

### Other related notes
- Academic/industrial 3D-printed rolling-diaphragm *cylinders* exist (e.g., the 2019 Trento paper “Low-Cost 3D Printed Rolling-Diaphragm Cylinders for Accurate Force Control”), but they typically embed commercial Dia·Com (or similar) molded rolling diaphragms rather than printing the flexible membrane itself.
- True commercial rolling diaphragms (Bellofram, Dia-Com, etc.) are fabric-reinforced elastomer parts and are not normally available as printable STLs; they are bought as finished seals.

If you’re looking for a pure TPU-printed membrane, a different geometry, higher-pressure capability, or STEP/CAD source files, let me know more details and I can refine the search.
