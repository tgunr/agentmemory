### 🤖 Assistant — 2026-07-14T15:07:33Z

**Yes, you can generally use Mio during intermittent fasting.**

Mio liquid water enhancers are **zero-calorie**, with 0g total carbohydrates, 0g sugars, 0g fat, and 0g protein per serving (typically ½ tsp or about 2–4 mL, which flavors 8 oz of water). Multiple product listings and nutrition databases confirm this across standard flavors and varieties.<grok:render card_id="0c8497" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">1</argument></grok:render>

### Why It Doesn't Break Most Fasts
Because it provides no calories or macronutrients that the body can use for energy, Mio does **not** break a fast in the conventional sense (caloric intake, significant insulin spike from food, or exiting ketosis for most people). Many intermittent fasting resources and experts consider zero-calorie flavored waters like Mio acceptable during the fasting window, especially since staying hydrated is important and it can make plain water more appealing.<grok:render card_id="11b509" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">10</argument></grok:render>

### Important Caveats
Mio uses **artificial sweeteners** — primarily **sucralose** and **acesulfame potassium (Ace-K)** — along with acids (citric/malic), natural flavors, preservatives, and sometimes colors. 

- Some studies suggest these sweeteners can trigger a minor **cephalic phase insulin response** (your body tasting sweetness and releasing a small amount of insulin) or slightly affect blood sugar/insulin in certain people. Effects vary by individual.<grok:render card_id="216d54" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">33</argument></grok:render>
- There are also potential concerns about gut microbiome changes with regular use of artificial sweeteners, though evidence is mixed and not conclusive for short-term fasting use.
- **Different Mio lines**:
  - Standard flavors: Just flavor + sweeteners.
  - **Mio Vitamins/Hydrate**: Adds B vitamins (B3, B6, B12) and/or electrolytes — these are generally fine or even helpful during fasting.
  - **Mio Energy**: Adds caffeine (about 60 mg per serving) + B vitamins + sometimes taurine/guarana/ginseng. Caffeine is usually acceptable (and can help suppress appetite), but sensitive people might prefer to avoid it.

For **weight loss, metabolic flexibility, or general health** goals with intermittent fasting, Mio is widely regarded as compatible and useful. For **very strict therapeutic fasting** (e.g., maximizing autophagy, deep ketosis, or gut rest), some people prefer to stick with plain water, black coffee, or unsweetened tea to minimize any possible metabolic signals.

### Practical Tips
- **It helps adherence** — Many people find flavored water makes longer fasts easier and improves hydration.
- Always check the label on your specific bottle/flavor, as minor formulation differences exist.
- Start with a small amount to see how your body responds (energy, hunger, cravings, or how you feel during/after the fast).
- If you notice increased hunger, cravings, or stalls in progress, try cycling off it for a while.

**Bottom line**: For the vast majority of people doing intermittent fasting, Mio is a convenient, zero-calorie option that won’t meaningfully break your fast. It’s a practical tool rather than a perfect “clean” choice. If you have specific health conditions, diabetes, or very strict fasting goals, consult a doctor or registered dietitian for personalized advice.
