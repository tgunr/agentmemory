### 🤖 Assistant

Charring chili peppers is a great way to add a smoky, deep flavor and make them easier to peel. Here are the most common ways to do it:

#### **Over an Open Flame (Gas Stove)**
This is the fastest way to get a true "fire-roasted" flavor. Hold the chili with metal tongs directly over the flame of a gas burner. Turn it frequently until the skin is blackened and blistered all over.

#### **Under the Broiler**
Place your chilies on a baking sheet and put them in the oven on the top rack under the broiler. Watch them closely and turn them once the top side is charred and bubbly (this usually takes 3–5 minutes per side).

#### **On a Cast Iron Skillet or Comal**
Heat a dry skillet over medium-high heat. Place the chilies in the pan and let them sit until the skin blackens, turning them every few minutes. This is perfect if you don't have a gas stove but want that direct-heat char.

#### **On the Grill**
Toss the chilies directly onto a hot grill grate. This is ideal for bulk charring during a BBQ and adds an extra

layer of wood or charcoal smoke.

#### **The "Sweating" Secret**
Once the chilies are charred, immediately place them in a bowl and cover it with plastic wrap (or put them in a sealed paper bag) for about 10–15 minutes. The steam will loosen the charred skin, making it slide right off under a bit of running water or with a paper towel.

### 🤖 Assistant

Chile Relleno is a classic Mexican dish of roasted, stuffed, and fried poblano peppers. Since you're already looking into charring chilies, you're halfway there! 

#### **1. Char and Prep the Peppers**
Use large **Poblano peppers** (they have a mild heat and sturdy walls). Char the skins using the flame or broiler method mentioned earlier, let them "sweat" in a bag, and then gently peel off the burnt skin. Carefully make a small slit down the side and remove the seeds, keeping the stem intact if possible.

#### **2. The Filling**
Traditionally, they are stuffed with **Oaxaca cheese** or Monterey Jack because they melt beautifully. Some versions use *picadillo* (a savory ground meat filling with raisins and nuts). Don't overstuff them, or they will burst during frying.

#### **3. The Batter (The Secret Step)**
The hallmark of a great Chile Relleno is the fluffy, "soufflé-like" egg batter:
*   Separate 4 eggs.
*   Beat the egg whites with a pinch of salt until you get **stiff peaks**.
*   Gently fold the

beaten yolks back into the whites so the batter stays airy.

#### **4. Assembly and Frying**
*   Dust each stuffed pepper in a light coating of all-purpose flour (this helps the egg batter stick).
*   Dip the pepper into the fluffy egg batter.
*   Carefully lower it into about an inch of hot vegetable oil in a skillet.
*   Spoon a little hot oil over the top to set the batter, then flip once the bottom is golden brown.

#### **5. Serving**
Serve them immediately while the cheese is molten. They are traditionally topped with a light, warm **tomato-based caldillo sauce** (made from blended tomatoes, onions, garlic, and chicken broth) and served with a side of Mexican rice and beans.
