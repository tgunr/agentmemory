### 👤 User

Where did the word bus come from for a BestBuy?

---

### 🤖 Assistant

The term **bus** as used in a retail or technical context (like at Best Buy) is derived from the same Latin root as the vehicle: the word **omnibus**.

### The Etymology of "Bus"
The word "bus" is a "clipping" or shortening of the Latin word **omnibus**, which means "for all" or "for everyone" (the dative plural of *omnis*). 

*   **Public Transport:** In the 1820s, the term was first used in France for a large horse-drawn carriage designed to carry many unrelated people along a fixed route. It was a "carriage for all" [Etymonline](https://www.etymonline.com/word/bus).
*   **Technical/Computing:** In electronics and computing—which is why you hear it at a place like Best Buy—a "bus" refers to a communication system that transfers data between components inside a computer or between computers. It is called a bus because it acts as a common pathway that "carries" signals to "all" connected devices, much like a public bus carries all passengers along a single route [Facebook - Hey Audio

Student](https://www.facebook.com/groups/heyaudiostudent/posts/25054642544163899/).

### Why You Hear It at Best Buy
When employees at Best Buy talk about a "bus," they are usually referring to hardware specifications like:
*   **Front Side Bus (FSB):** The path that carries data between the CPU and the main memory.
*   **USB (Universal Serial Bus):** A "universal" connection "for all" types of peripherals (mice, keyboards, drives).
*   **PCI Bus:** The slots on a motherboard used to add graphics or sound cards.

In all these cases, the name reflects the original Latin meaning: a single, shared path designed to serve everyone (or every component) connected to it [Quora](https://www.quora.com/What-is-the-etymology-of-the-word-bus-Why-do-we-call-them-buses-instead-of-autobuses-or-omnibuses-Why-isnt-there-a-more-specific-term-for-these-vehicles-like-the-way-we-differentiate-between-trucks-and-cars).

### 👤 User

As a BestBuy at a restaurant called the bus where did they come from?

---

### 🤖 Assistant

In a restaurant, **“the bus”** (and verbs like **“to bus a table”**) comes from the same root as the vehicle word **bus**:

- **bus** is short for **omnibus** (“for all”), and it entered English as the name for a passenger vehicle in the 1800s. From that noun, English later made the verb **to bus** (“to transport / carry”), and then it picked up the restaurant-specific sense **“to clear tables”** (late 1800s), because staff would carry dishes away—often using a **bus tub** or **bus cart**. That’s also where **busboy** comes from.  
  Source: [Online Etymology Dictionary – bus](https://www.etymonline.com/word/bus)

So when someone at a restaurant says “I’m on the bus” or “I’m bussing,” they mean **clearing and carrying dishes**, and the term ultimately traces back to **bus ← omnibus**.
