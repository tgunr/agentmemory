### 👤 User

Why do some chickens make green eggs shells

---

### 🤖 Assistant

Chickens lay green eggs due to a specific combination of genetics and pigments applied during the egg-forming process. While all eggs start out white, the final color is determined by the hen's breed and the specific pigments her body produces.

### The "Blue + Brown" Formula
The most common reason for a green eggshell is the combination of two different pigments:
*   **Biliverdin (Blue):** Some breeds possess a dominant gene that deposits a pigment called oocyanin (a byproduct of bile) early in the shell-forming process. This tints the entire shell blue, inside and out.
*   **Protoporphyrin (Brown):** Other breeds deposit a brown pigment on the surface of the shell during the final hours before the egg is laid.

When a chicken has the genetics for both—meaning it produces a blue shell but then "paints" a layer of brown pigment over it—the result is a green or olive-colored egg. Because the brown is only a surface coating, if you scratch a green egg, you might see blue underneath, and the inside of the shell

will typically appear blue.

### Common Breeds That Lay Green Eggs
Several specific breeds and hybrids are famous for their green-tinted baskets:
*   **Easter Eggers:** These are popular hybrid chickens (often a cross involving an Ameraucana or Araucana) that can lay a variety of colors, including aqua, blue, and green.
*   **Olive Eggers:** These are specifically bred by crossing a blue-egg layer (like a Cream Legbar) with a dark-brown-egg layer (like a Marans). The result is a deep, "olive" green shell.
*   **Araucanas:** A breed from South America that is one of the original sources of the blue/green egg gene.
*   **Favaucanas:** A newer cross between Faverolles and Ameraucanas that often produces sage-green eggs.

### Key Facts About Green Eggs
*   **Nutrition:** Despite the exotic color, green eggs have the [same nutritional value](https://hatchingtime.com/blogs/home/why-do-chickens-lay-different-colored-eggs) as white or brown eggs. The color is purely cosmetic and does not affect the taste or quality

of the egg.
*   **Consistency:** Once a hen starts laying a certain color, she will lay that same color for her entire life, though the shade may lighten slightly as she gets older or during periods of high production.
*   **Inside the Egg:** The yolk and white of a green egg look exactly like those of any other chicken egg.

For more detailed information on poultry genetics and egg colors, you can refer to resources like [Hatching Time](https://hatchingtime.com/blogs/home/why-do-chickens-lay-different-colored-eggs), [BackYard Chickens](https://www.backyardchickens.com/threads/what-causes-green-eggs.695603/), and [Sauder's Eggs](https://saudereggs.com/blog/what-do-different-eggshell-colors-mean/).
