### 👤 User

Can Mica powder be used in laser engraving? At what temperature does it melt? If not what kinds of powder paint can be fused by laser and at what settings?

---

### 🤖 Assistant

Mica powder is generally **not recommended** for direct laser engraving because it does not melt or fuse under the laser beam in a way that creates a permanent bond. Instead, it typically remains a loose powder or vaporizes without adhering to the surface.

### Mica Powder and Laser Engraving
Mica is a mineral with a very high melting point, often exceeding $1200^{\circ}C$ ($2192^{\circ}F$). In laser tests, mica-based materials like Micanite have shown resistance to temperatures up to $800^{\circ}C$ or even $1000^{\circ}C$ for short periods [eurolaser](https://www.eurolaser.com/applications/laser-cutting-of-micanite). 

Because the laser cannot easily melt mica into a liquid state that bonds with the substrate, users often find that the powder can simply be brushed away after the laser pass [LA Hobby Guy](https://lahobbyguy.com/bb/viewtopic.php?t=5177). While some people use mica for "inlays" by engraving a deep pocket and then filling it with mica mixed with epoxy or CA glue, the laser itself does not

"fuse" the mica to the material [Penturners](https://www.penturners.org/threads/anyone-use-mica-powder-here.162290/).

### Recommended Alternative: Powder Coating
If you want to fuse a colored powder to a surface using a laser, **Powder Coating Paint** (thermoplastic powder) is the standard choice. These powders are designed to melt and flow at much lower temperatures, typically between **$375^{\circ}F$ and $400^{\circ}F$** ($190^{\circ}C$ to $205^{\circ}C$) [Reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/Laserengraving/comments/10zie7r/powdercoating_with_laser_help_please_more_info_in/).

#### Fusing Settings and Tips
When using powder coating for laser "inlays" or coloring, the goal is to provide just enough heat to melt the plastic media without vaporizing it or burning the substrate.

*   **Power and Speed:** High speeds and low power are generally required. For example, users have reported success on diode lasers using speeds around **4000–5000 mm/min** with power levels between **17% and 20%** [LA Hobby

Guy](https://lahobbyguy.com/bb/viewtopic.php?t=1429). If the laser is too slow or too powerful, the powder will "flash off" (vaporize) rather than melting into a smooth finish.
*   **Air Assist:** You should **turn off air assist** or use a very low setting, as the air will blow the light powder away before the laser can melt it.
*   **The Glass Trick:** A common technique to prevent the powder from blowing away (especially from the laser's internal cooling fan) is to place a thin sheet of clear glass (like from a picture frame) over the powder-covered workpiece [LA Hobby Guy](https://lahobbyguy.com/bb/viewtopic.php?t=1429).
*   **Material Types:** Polyester-based powders are the most common and generally yield the best results for this application. Epoxy-based powders can be more difficult as they may burn or fail to melt properly under the laser [LA Hobby Guy](https://lahobbyguy.com/bb/viewtopic.php?t=1429).
