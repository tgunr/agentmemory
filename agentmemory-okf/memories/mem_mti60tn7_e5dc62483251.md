What are some appropriate forage grasses for cattle and goats in East Texas near Houston?

---

### 🤖 Assistant — 2025-03-10T15:23:13Z

For East Texas near Houston, you'll need forage grasses that can handle the warm, humid climate with periodic droughts. Here are some appropriate options for both cattle and goats:

For cattle:
- Bermudagrass (especially Coastal, Tifton 85, and Jiggs varieties) - excellent heat tolerance and productivity
- Bahiagrass - very drought resistant and grows well in sandy soils
- Dallisgrass - adapts well to the heavy clay soils common in parts of East Texas
- Crabgrass - provides good summer forage
- Eastern Gamagrass - native perennial that handles wet conditions well

For goats:
- Bermudagrass - goats prefer it when it's young and tender
- Bahiagrass - provides reliable forage for browsing
- Pearl Millet - warm-season annual that goats find palatable
- Sericea Lespedeza - has the added benefit of natural parasite control properties for goats

For both:
- Ryegrass (annual) - excellent cool-season option for winter grazing
- Small grains (oats, wheat, rye) - good for winter and early spring
- Clovers (crimson, arrowleaf, white) - nitrogen-fixing legumes that complement grass pastures

The East Texas climate near Houston allows for year-round grazing with proper management by combining warm-season perennials with cool-season annuals. Soil testing before planting is recommended since soil types vary considerably in the region.

Is Sericea Lespedeza suitable for cattle?

---

### 🤖 Assistant — 2025-03-10T15:24:45Z

Sericea Lespedeza is generally not an ideal forage for cattle. While goats can benefit from it due to its natural parasite control properties and their ability to browse more effectively, cattle have different preferences and nutritional needs.

For cattle, Sericea Lespedeza has several drawbacks:

- High tannin content makes it unpalatable to cattle, especially as it matures
- Lower digestibility compared to other legumes like clovers or alfalfa
- Cattle tend to avoid it when other forages are available
- Can become stemmy and woody, further reducing cattle consumption

If you're looking for legumes better suited for cattle in East Texas, consider:

- White clover
- Crimson clover
- Arrowleaf clover
- Annual lespedeza varieties (Korean or Kobe) which have lower tannin levels
- Alfalfa (though challenging in East Texas humidity)

These alternatives provide better palatability, digestibility, and nutritional value for cattle while still offering the nitrogen-fixing benefits of legumes in your pasture system.

### 🤖 Assistant — 2025-03-10T15:25:30Z

You're absolutely right to be concerned about bloat with clovers in cattle. Bloat is a serious digestive issue that can be fatal if not treated quickly.

Clovers (particularly white clover and red clover) can indeed cause bloat in cattle because:
- They're highly digestible and ferment rapidly in the rumen
- They contain soluble proteins that create a stable foam in the rumen
- This foam traps gas that cattle can't expel through normal belching

However, clovers can still be used with proper management:

1. Never turn hungry cattle onto lush clover pastures
2. Introduce cattle to clover gradually to allow rumen microbes to adapt
3. Plant clover-grass mixtures rather than pure clover stands (aim for less than 50% clover)
4. Avoid grazing clover during early morning when dew is present
5. Consider using bloat-reducing supplements like poloxalene
6. Some newer clover varieties have been bred to reduce bloat risk
