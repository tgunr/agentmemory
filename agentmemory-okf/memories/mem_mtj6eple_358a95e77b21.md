### 🤖 Assistant — 2026-08-22T18:56:46Z

**For a minor shiny spot / tiny crater or high spot on a flood-coat epoxy tabletop:**

### 1. Diagnose first
- Is it a **high spot** (epoxy that cured thicker in one place) or a **low spot / pin-hole / crater**?
- From the photo it looks like a small raised or glossy area that catches the light differently — very common on flood coats.

### 2. Best light-touch fix (preferred for something this small)

**Option A – Wet sand + polish (safest and most common)**  
1. Mask off the surrounding area with painter’s tape so you only work on the blemish.  
2. Start with **600–800 grit** wet sandpaper (use water + a drop of dish soap). Sand **gently in circles or with the grain** just until the shine is gone and the surface feels level.  
3. Move up through **1000 → 1500 → 2000 grit** (still wet).  
4. Dry thoroughly.  
5. Polish with a fine plastic/epoxy polish or automotive compound (Meguiar’s PlastX, Novus 2, or a dedicated epoxy polish) using a soft microfiber or foam pad.  
6. Finish with a clean microfiber for a uniform sheen.

This removes almost no material and restores the glass-like finish without having to recoat the whole top.

### 3. If it’s an actual crater / pin-hole
- Clean the spot well.  
- Mix a tiny amount of the **same epoxy** (or a clear UV-stable topcoat epoxy).  
- Drop it into the hole with a toothpick so it slightly overfills.  
- Let it cure fully, then wet-sand and polish level as above.

### 4. Things to avoid
- Dry sanding (creates heat and scratches that are harder to polish out).  
- Aggressive coarse grit (120–220) — overkill for a minor blemish and risks a visible low spot.  
- Trying to “buff it out” with only polish if it’s raised — you’ll just shine the high spot more.

Would you rather keep the current high-gloss look, or are you planning a satin/matte finish overall? That changes whether you stop at polishing or continue to a finer grit + matte compound.

**Wet-sand only the high spot, then polish it back.** No need to recoat the whole top for something this minor.

1. **Prep**  
   - Clean the area with isopropyl alcohol or a mild detergent wipe and dry completely.  
   - Mask tightly around the spot with painter’s tape so you don’t scuff the surrounding gloss.

   - Use **600–800 grit** wet/dry sandpaper on a small foam or rubber sanding block (or just fold the paper over a fingertip for ultra-light control).
