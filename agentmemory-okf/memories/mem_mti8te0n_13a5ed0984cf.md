### 🤖 Assistant

It is a delicate balance to ask a company to open-source proprietary code, especially when their primary concern is the "maintenance tax"—the time and resources required to manage a community. Your idea of a "soft launch" (releasing the code but not immediately committing to PR management) is a very practical starting point.

To make this request more compelling, you should frame it as a strategic business advantage rather than just a favor to the community. Here is a refined version of your request, followed by the rational arguments that support it.

### Proposed Draft

**Subject: Proposal for a Community-Led Open Source Strategy for [Software Name]**

"I am writing to share a proposal regarding the future of [Software Name]. I still sincerely believe there is a significant opportunity to open-source specific components of the codebase to allow the community to assist in its evolution.

I completely understand the reluctance to do so, particularly the concern that internal engineers would need to be

diverted to examine and integrate pull requests. To mitigate this, I suggest a 'Source-Available' or 'Passive Open Source' initial phase:

1.  **Low-Overhead Launch:** Post the source code and build instructions to GitHub with a clear disclaimer that the repository is currently 'unmanaged' regarding external PRs.
2.  **Community Self-Sorting:** This allows the community to fork the project, experiment, and fix bugs independently. Those of us with the technical expertise can vet and merge PRs into our own forks.
3.  **Organic Leadership:** Over time, one or more community forks will naturally become the 'gold standard' due to high-quality maintenance.
4.  **Internal Benefit:** Once a community fork has matured and been 'battle-tested' by the public, your team can selectively pull those verified improvements back into the internal codebase with significantly less effort than developing them from scratch.

This approach allows [Company Name] to reap the benefits of community innovation without the immediate

burden of active repository management."

### Rational and Compelling Arguments

To strengthen your case, you can include these points in your discussion or as a "Benefits" section in your email:

#### 1. Reduced R&D and Maintenance Costs
By open-sourcing, the company effectively gains a global team of "volunteer" QA testers and developers. Bug fixes for edge cases that your internal team might never encounter are often submitted by users who are personally affected by them, saving the company hours of troubleshooting.

#### 2. Extended Product Longevity
Software often dies because the internal team's roadmap shifts. Open-sourcing ensures that the product remains viable and useful to customers even if the company decides to pivot its primary focus elsewhere. This builds immense brand loyalty and trust.
