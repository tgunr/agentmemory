Compare dot-most with redmine as an alternative to using Atlassian Confluence

### 🤖 Assistant

If you’re looking for a Confluence alternative, **dot-most** and **Redmine** sit in pretty different categories—so the comparison is less “which is better” and more “which fits your workflow.”

Here’s a clear breakdown:

---

## 🧠 Core positioning

- **dot-most**
  - Built as a **modern knowledge base / documentation tool**
  - Much closer in spirit to **Confluence, Notion, or Outline**
  - Focus: clean docs, collaboration, internal wikis

- **Redmine**
  - Primarily a **project management + issue tracking system**
  - Wiki is **secondary**, not the main feature
  - Focus: tickets, roadmaps, dev workflows

👉 If your goal is to replace Confluence specifically, dot-most is the more direct match.

---

## ✍️ Documentation & editing experience

- **dot-most**
  - Rich, modern editor (Markdown or WYSIWYG depending on setup)
  - Clean UI, better readability
  - Designed for structured knowledge sharing

- **Redmine**
  - Basic wiki editor (textile/markdown depending on config)
  - Feels dated
  - Not ideal for

large documentation systems

👉 Redmine works for simple docs, but it’s not enjoyable for heavy documentation.

---

## 🔗 Organization & navigation

- **dot-most**
  - Hierarchical pages (like Confluence spaces/pages)
  - Better search and linking
  - Designed for discoverability

- **Redmine**
  - Wiki pages per project
  - Limited structure and navigation
  - Search is functional but not great

---

## 👥 Collaboration

- **dot-most**
  - Real-time or near real-time collaboration (depending on deployment)
  - Comments, versioning, clean diffing

- **Redmine**
  - Collaboration happens more through **issues**, not docs
  - Wiki collaboration is basic

---

## 🔧 Features beyond documentation

- **dot-most**
  - Focused on docs (may include auth, permissions, integrations)
  - Not a project tracker

- **Redmine**
  - Strong:
    - Issue tracking
    - Gantt charts
    - Time tracking
    - Agile plugins
  - Weak as a knowledge base

👉 Redmine is closer to Jira than Confluence.

---

## 🧩 Extensibility

-

**dot-most**
  - Depends on ecosystem (smaller, newer)
  - Likely fewer plugins than Redmine

- **Redmine**
  - Huge plugin ecosystem
  - Very customizable (especially for dev teams)

---

## 🖥️ UI/UX

- **dot-most**
  - Modern, clean, Confluence-like feel

- **Redmine**
  - Functional but dated
  - Requires theming to feel modern

---

## 🏁 When to choose which

### Choose **dot-most** if:
- You want a **true Confluence replacement**
- Documentation is a **core workflow**
- You care about UX and readability
- You don’t need heavy issue tracking

### Choose **Redmine** if:
- You need **Jira + lightweight wiki**
- Your team is **engineering-heavy**
- Documentation is secondary to task tracking
- You want strong open-source PM tooling

---

## 💡 Bottom line

- **dot-most = Confluence alternative**
- **Redmine = Jira alternative (with a basic wiki)**

If you try to use Redmine as a Confluence replacement, it’ll feel like forcing a square peg into a round hole.

---

other stronger Confluence alternatives (like Outline, BookStack, or Wiki.js), which might be closer competitors depending on your setup.
