In a criminal jury trial does the jury always have to be unanimous?

---

### 🤖 Assistant

In a criminal jury trial, the jury generally has to be unanimous to reach a verdict. If the jury cannot reach a unanimous decision, it's considered a "hung jury," and the judge will usually declare a mistrial. This means the prosecution can decide to retry the case with a new jury.

### 👤 User

What is meant by a jury of your peers?

---

### 🤖 Assistant

The phrase "jury of your peers" refers to a fundamental concept in many legal systems, particularly in common law countries like the United States and the United Kingdom. It essentially means that a person accused of a crime has the right to be tried by a jury made up of ordinary citizens who are considered to be their equals or fellow members of the community.

Here's a breakdown of what that generally implies:

*   **Representative of the Community:** The idea is that the jury should be a fair cross-section of the community where the trial is taking place. This doesn't mean the jury has to be identical to the defendant in every demographic aspect, but rather that the selection process should not systematically exclude any particular group.
*   **Impartiality:** The "peers" are expected to be impartial and unbiased, capable of listening to the evidence and applying the law without prejudice.
*   **Ordinary Citizens:** Jurors are typically not legal experts, but rather everyday people who bring their common

sense and life experiences to the deliberation process.
*   **Protection Against Oppression:** The concept serves as a safeguard against potential government overreach or biased legal proceedings. By having ordinary citizens decide guilt or innocence, it theoretically prevents a single judge or a politically motivated system from unjustly convicting someone.

It's important to note that while the ideal is a jury of one's peers, the practical application can sometimes be complex. Jury selection processes aim to achieve this ideal, but challenges can arise in ensuring true representativeness and impartiality.
