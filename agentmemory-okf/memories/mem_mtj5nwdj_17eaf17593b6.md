What do you get when you mix a Aberdeen Angus with a Brangus?

---

### 🤖 Assistant

When you cross an **Aberdeen Angus** (a purebred Angus) with a **Brangus** (a mix of Angus and Brahman), you get a hybrid that is **approximately 75% Angus and 25% Brahman**, assuming the Brangus parent is the standard 5/8 Angus and 3/8 Brahman.

This cross would enhance:
- **Meat quality** from the high Angus genetics.
- **Heat tolerance and disease resistance** from the Brahman influence in the Brangus.
- **Maternal traits** like good mothering ability and calving ease.

The resulting cattle would look and behave similarly to purebred Angus but might have slightly looser skin, some hump development, and better heat adaptability than a pure Angus.

What about mixing an Aberdeen Angus with a Dexter?

---

### 🤖 Assistant

Crossing an **Aberdeen Angus** with a **Dexter** results in a small to mid-sized beef animal that retains many desirable traits from both breeds.  

### **Expected Characteristics of an Angus-Dexter Cross:**
- **Size:** Smaller than a full-size Angus but larger than a Dexter, making them more efficient for small farms or limited grazing areas.  
- **Meat Quality:** Likely to have excellent marbling from the Angus genetics while retaining the rich, flavorful beef Dexter cattle are known for.  
- **Hardiness:** Dexter cattle are very hardy and can thrive in rougher conditions. Crossing with Angus may enhance resilience while maintaining good foraging ability.  
- **Temperament:** Both breeds are generally docile, making the cross easier to handle.  
- **Calving Ease:** Dexter cattle are known for easy calving, and Angus also has good calving traits. The cross should inherit this advantage.  
- **Milk Production:** If using a Dexter cow, the cross may inherit good milk production, making females useful for dual-purpose (beef and milk).  

This mix is great for small-scale beef production or homesteaders who want hardy, efficient cattle with excellent meat quality.
