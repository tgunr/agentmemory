### 🤖 Assistant — 2026-08-18T19:11:09Z

**The easiest way for a sole proprietor to get paid from a government credit card (usually a GSA SmartPay Purchase Card / P-card) is simply to accept regular Visa or Mastercard payments.** Government purchase cards run on the same Visa/Mastercard networks as commercial cards. If you can process a normal credit card, you can process a government one—no special government merchant account or extra approval is required for most small purchases.<grok:render card_id="8b5f74" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">35</argument></grok:render>

### Why This Works Easily
- **No SAM.gov registration needed** for typical micro-purchases (generally under $10,000–$15,000 depending on the current threshold and agency). Buyers can just use the card with any vendor who accepts Visa/Mastercard.<grok:render card_id="438428" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">17</argument></grok:render>
- Government cards are charge cards (paid in full by the agency monthly). Payment risk is very low.
- Sole proprietors can sign up for payment processors using just a Social Security Number (or a free EIN) and a bank account. No LLC or formal business entity is required.

### Easiest Practical Options (Ranked by Simplicity)

1. **Square (usually the simplest overall)**  
   - Sign up as a sole proprietor with your SSN.  
   - Free magstripe reader available; low-cost contactless/chip readers.  
   - In-person rates around 2.6% + 15¢; online a bit higher.  
   - Instant or next-day deposits to your bank account.  
   - Works for both face-to-face and online/invoicing.  
   Great for services, products, or mobile work.

2. **Stripe**  
   - Excellent for online payments, invoices, or websites.  
   - Sign up as individual/sole proprietor.  
   - Clear pricing (~2.9% + 30¢).  
   - Strong invoicing and payment-link tools.

3. **PayPal Business**  
   - Very fast to set up.  
   - Send invoices or payment requests by email/link.  
   - Buyers can pay with a credit card even without a PayPal account.  
   - Convenient for remote or service-based work.

Other options (Helcim, traditional bank merchant accounts, etc.) exist but usually involve more paperwork or monthly fees, so they are less “easy” for starting out.

### Important Tips When Taking a Government Card
- **Tax exemption**: Most GSA SmartPay Purchase Cards are Centrally Billed Accounts and are exempt from state sales tax. Do not charge sales tax. Ask the cardholder to identify it as an official government purchase (they may show a government ID or tax-exempt form). Check the GSA SmartPay tax info by state if needed.<grok:render card_id="6c4d4b" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">66</argument></grok:render>
- **Receipts**: Always provide a clear itemized receipt or invoice.
- **Third-party processors** (Square, PayPal, Stripe): These work for many micro-purchases, especially with known vendors. Some agencies view them as higher-risk because the merchant name can be less transparent, so a direct processor is preferred when possible—but they are still widely used.
- **Merchant Category Code (MCC)**: Rarely an issue, but if a card is declined, your business category code might be blocked by that agency. Contact your processor to review/update it.

### Optional Steps That Make Getting More Government Work Easier
- Get a free EIN from the IRS (irs.gov) so your SSN isn’t tied to every payment.
- Open a separate business bank account (even as a sole prop) for cleaner bookkeeping.
- Register for free in SAM.gov if you want larger contracts
