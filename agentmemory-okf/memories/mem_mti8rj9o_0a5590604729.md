### 👤 User

What famous British landmark was moved to Arizona

---

### 🤖 Assistant

The famous British landmark that was moved to Arizona is the **London Bridge**.

The bridge currently spans the Bridgewater Channel in **Lake Havasu City, Arizona**. It was originally built in the 1830s to cross the River Thames in London, England, but by the 1960s, it was literally "falling down"—it was sinking into the riverbed at a rate of about an inch every eight years because it could not handle the weight of modern traffic.

#### Key Facts about the Relocation:
*   **The Purchase:** In 1968, American entrepreneur and founder of Lake Havasu City, **Robert P. McCulloch**, purchased the bridge from the City of London for **$2,460,000**.
*   **The Move:** The bridge was dismantled piece by piece. Each of the **10,276 exterior granite blocks** was numbered to ensure they could be reassembled correctly. The stones were shipped through the Panama Canal to California and then trucked to the Arizona desert.
*   **Reconstruction:** The bridge was rebuilt over a land isthmus using a modern steel-reinforced

concrete structure, which was then clad in the original 19th-century granite. After completion, a channel was dredged underneath it to allow water to flow through, effectively turning a peninsula into an island.
*   **The "Tower Bridge" Myth:** A popular urban legend suggests McCulloch thought he was buying the more iconic, turreted **Tower Bridge**. However, both McCulloch and the city officials involved in the sale have consistently denied this, stating it was always known which bridge was being sold.

The bridge was formally dedicated on October 10, 1971, and remains one of Arizona's most popular tourist attractions. You can find more details about its history and visiting the site at [Go Lake Havasu](https://www.golakehavasu.com/london-bridge/) and [HISTORY](https://www.history.com/articles/how-london-bridge-ended-up-in-arizona).

---

### 🤖 Assistant

On average, **England has roughly 130 to 150 rainy days per year**, depending on how “rainy day” is defined and which part of England you mean.

A few useful nuances:

- **Western and northern areas** usually have **more** rainy days.
- **Southeastern England** tends to have **fewer**.
- Meteorologists often count a “rain day” as a day with at least **1 mm of precipitation**, so drizzle/light rain may or may not be included depending on the source.
