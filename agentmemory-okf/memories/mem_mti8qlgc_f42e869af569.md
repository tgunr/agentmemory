### 🤖 Assistant

When choosing a hardwood for a cutting board, the goal is to find a balance between durability (resisting deep gouges) and "knife-friendliness" (being soft enough not to dull your blades quickly). The "holy trinity" of cutting board woods consistently recommended by experts and enthusiasts consists of Maple, Walnut, and Cherry.

### The Top Three Recommendations

*   **Maple (Hard Maple):** Often considered the gold standard, maple is a highly durable, closed-grain hardwood. Its tight pores make it naturally sanitary by resisting moisture and bacterial growth. It has a Janka hardness rating of around 1,450, providing a sturdy surface that still yields slightly to high-end knives [Misen](https://misen.com/blogs/news/best-wood-for-cutting-board-a-comprehensive-guide).
*   **Walnut (Black Walnut):** This is a popular choice for those who prefer a darker aesthetic. Walnut is slightly softer than maple (Janka rating of ~1,010), which makes it very gentle on knife edges. Its deep color is also excellent for hiding

stains and minor knife marks [Arnold Wood Turning](https://www.arnoldwood.com/blog/best-woods-for-cutting-boards-a-bulk-buyers-guide-to-quality-and-consistency/).
*   **Cherry:** Cherry is a softer hardwood that develops a beautiful reddish-brown patina over time. While it will show knife marks more easily than maple, it is exceptionally easy on blades. It is a great choice for boards used for lighter prep or as attractive serving platters [Serious Eats](https://www.seriouseats.com/best-wooden-cutting-boards).

### Other Notable Options

*   **Larch (Tamarack):** Often used in high-quality end-grain boards, larch is naturally rot-resistant and has a unique "self-healing" quality due to its fibrous structure [Serious Eats](https://www.seriouseats.com/best-wooden-cutting-boards).
*   **Teak:** Teak is high in natural oils, making it very resistant to water and warping. However, it contains naturally occurring silica, which can be abrasive and may dull your knives faster than maple or walnut [chefknives on

Reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/chefknives/comments/kqjurz/best_wood_for_cutting_board/).
*   **Acacia:** A more budget-friendly alternative that is dense and durable. Like teak, some species of acacia have higher silica content, so while durable, it may require more frequent knife sharpening [Serious Eats](https://www.seriouseats.com/best-wooden-cutting-boards).

### Key Factors for Selection

*   **Grain Type:** End-grain boards (where the wood fibers stand vertically) are the most durable and gentle on knives because the blade slides between the fibers. Edge-grain boards (fibers run horizontally) are generally less expensive but more prone to showing deep gashes over time [Serious Eats](https://www.seriouseats.com/best-wooden-cutting-boards).
*   **Porosity:** Always choose "closed-grain" hardwoods. Avoid open-pored woods like Oak or Ash, as their large pores can trap food particles and bacteria, making them difficult to sanitize [Arnold Wood

Turning](https://www.arnoldwood.com/blog/best-woods-for-cutting-boards-a-bulk-buyers-guide-to-quality-and-consistency/).
*   **Toxicity:** Stick to woods from trees that produce edible fruits, nuts, or sap. Avoid exotic woods may contain natural toxins or sensitizing oils that are not food-safe [Arnold Wood Turning](https://www.arnoldwood.com/blog/best-woods-for-cutting-boards-a-bulk-buyers-guide-to-quality-and-consistency/).
