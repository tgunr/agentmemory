### 🤖 Assistant

It is always tricky to balance a friendship with a landlord-tenant or employer-employee dynamic. To be friendly but firm, you should focus on the impact his actions have on the arrangement rather than attacking his character, while clearly outlining the new structure you need.

Here is a rephrased version that upholds your boundaries:

"James, I value our friendship and I'm genuinely glad you’ve found full-time work. However, the current arrangement for the property isn't working out as we agreed. As you know, honesty, fulfillment of duties, and communication were the three pillars I needed for this to work. While I appreciate your honesty, the lack of follow-through and communication on tasks has become a significant stressor for me.

Because I need these jobs completed reliably, I’m moving to a strictly monetary arrangement to keep things clear and professional. Starting [Date], I’ll need you to pay the $400 monthly rent plus your electricity costs. To make this fair, I would still love to hire you to do

the work around the property, but we will treat it as a separate job where I pay you for specific tasks completed. This ensures the work gets done and simplifies our agreement. If you have another suggestion that ensures the work is completed and communication is consistent, I’m open to listening, but we do need to make this change immediately."

#### Key points this version addresses:

*   **Reiterates expectations:** It reminds him of the "three pillars" (honesty, fulfillment, and communication) without sounding like a lecture.
*   **Removes the "teenager" dynamic:** By switching to a cash-based system, you remove the need to "nag" him. If he doesn't do the work, he simply doesn't get paid, rather than you losing out on rent.
*   **Sets a clear boundary on utilities:** It mentions the electricity without sounding like you are holding a grudge, but makes it clear that the "free ride" on that expense is ending.
*   **Invites collaboration:** Ending with an invitation to hear his ideas keeps the "friendly"

bridge open while standing your ground on the "firm" necessity of a change.
