This is within my knowledge and doesn't require searching since these are established woodworking techniques.For a bathroom application with white oak, you'll need a multi-layered approach to minimize wood movement. Here are the key techniques:

**Wood Preparation:**
- Ensure your white oak is kiln-dried to 6-8% moisture content before starting
- Allow the lumber to acclimate in your shop for at least a week before milling
- Consider quartersawn stock if available, as it's naturally more stable than plain-sawn

**Construction Techniques:**
- Use alternating grain direction when gluing up sections - alternate the end grain pattern (cathedral up, cathedral down) to counteract cupping tendencies
- Keep individual strips relatively narrow (3-4 inches max) rather than using wide sections
- Use a high-quality waterproof glue like Titebond III or epoxy
- Consider a torsion box construction with a stable core (MDF or plywood) and white oak veneer on both faces

**Moisture Management:**
- Apply finish to ALL surfaces, including edges and the back face - this is crucial for moisture equilibrium
- Use a high-quality marine or exterior-grade polyurethane, or consider a penetrating oil finish like Watco Teak Oil
- Apply multiple thin coats rather than fewer thick ones
- Pay special attention to end grain sealing

**Design Considerations:**
- Allow for controlled movement by using floating panel construction if this is part of a cabinet door
- Consider breadboard ends with elongated holes for fasteners
- Ensure proper ventilation around the panel

**Alternative Approach:**
Consider using a plywood substrate with white oak veneer. This gives you the appearance you want with dramatically improved stability in humid conditions.
