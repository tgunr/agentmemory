### Short answer

Based on the visible caption from the Facebook reel, the claim looks **partly true but heavily exaggerated / misleading**.

The reel says things like **“Cooking on most pans is not safe”** and that pans are **“stripping vital nutrients from your food.”** That overstates the evidence.

### What’s true

- **Overheating some nonstick pans can be a problem.**  
  PTFE/Teflon-type coatings can start breaking down at very high temperatures and release fumes, especially if a pan is left empty on high heat. Sources like [Healthline](https://www.healthline.com/nutrition/nonstick-cookware-safety) and [WebMD](https://www.webmd.com/food-recipes/is-teflon-coating-safe) describe this risk.

- **Older nonstick cookware raised legitimate concerns about PFOA/PFAS.**  
  PFOA used in manufacturing was a real concern, though newer Teflon products have been PFOA-free since 2013 according to [Healthline](https://www.healthline.com/nutrition/nonstick-cookware-safety). The broader PFAS issue is also real, per the

[American Cancer Society](https://www.cancer.org/cancer/risk-prevention/chemicals/teflon-and-perfluorooctanoic-acid-pfoa.html).

- **“Non-toxic” cookware marketing is not always trustworthy.**  
  [Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/toxic-chemicals-substances/you-cant-always-trust-claims-on-non-toxic-cookware-a4849321487/) found that some cookware claims can be misleading.

### What’s misleading or likely false

- **“Most pans are unsafe”** — not supported.  
  Mainstream health guidance does **not** say most cookware is dangerous. In fact, the [American Cancer Society](https://www.cancer.org/cancer/risk-prevention/chemicals/teflon-and-perfluorooctanoic-acid-pfoa.html) says there are **no proven risks to humans from using cookware coated with Teflon/non-stick surfaces** under normal use.

- **“Pans strip vital nutrients from food”** — misleading.  
  Nutrient loss is mostly about **cooking method, temperature, time, and water use**, not the simple fact that food touched a pan. High heat and

overcooking can reduce some nutrients, but that is very different from saying “most pans strip nutrients.”

### Best evidence-based takeaway

A more accurate version would be:

> **Some nonstick pans can release fumes if overheated, and PFAS-related concerns are real enough that some people prefer stainless steel, cast iron, or ceramic. But most cookware is not inherently unsafe, and the claim that pans broadly strip vital nutrients is overstated.**

### Practical advice

If you use nonstick cookware:

- don’t preheat it empty on high heat
- use low/medium heat when possible
- replace pans that are badly scratched, peeling, or flaking
- use wood/silicone utensils
- if you want to avoid PFAS concerns entirely, use stainless steel, cast iron, or verified PTFE-free options
