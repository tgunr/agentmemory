# Summary

The reference explains how a BlueBubbles outbound message can echo back into Hermes as inbound traffic and create a self-loop. The robust solution combines reserved-channel filtering with short-lived, normalized text correlation across multiple chat identifier forms, while allowing legitimate repeated messages after the 30-second window.

## Facts
- On macOS Tahoe (26) with helper_connected=false, outbound echoes can arrive both through the reserved any;- channel and the genuine chat GUID, making a channel-only guard insufficient.
- The working guard records (chat, normalized_text) timestamps after successful sends under both the handle and resolved GUID, then suppresses matching webhook text within a 30-second window.
- Text normalization trims, lowercases, and collapses whitespace; webhook candidate IDs include session_chat_id, chat_identifier, chat_guid, and prefix-stripped forms.
- Defense layers also drop any;- channels and Hermes status messages prefixed with 💭, ⚡, 🤔, or 🔧.
- GUID-only suppression failed because AppleScript fallback may return guid="ok" and inbound echo GUIDs may differ; any;- suppression alone failed because most echoes used the real chat GUID.
- Tests cover recent normalized-text suppression on a real chat, expiration after 999 seconds, any;- filtering, and status-prefix filtering.
- Emergency mitigation is to stop the BlueBubbles launchctl application, then relaunch BlueBubbles and restart the Hermes gateway after deploying the fix.
