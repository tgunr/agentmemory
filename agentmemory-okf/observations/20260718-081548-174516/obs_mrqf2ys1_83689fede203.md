# Summary

Retrieved reference documentation for UV printing on wood substrates. The guide covers critical sealing requirements for maple/hardwood before UV printing, emphasizing the distinction between sealer (print quality) and epoxy (protection), and specifying dewaxed clear shellac as the only appropriate sealer to avoid fish eyes and color shifting under epoxy flood coats.

## Facts
- File: references/wood.md, skill: uv-printing
- Wood is porous and absorbent; raw wood causes muted color, lost detail, and uneven tone in UV prints
- Sealer provides print-quality surface; epoxy flood coat provides final protection — they are different jobs
- Recommended sealer: dewaxed clear shellac, 2-lb cut (e.g. Zinsser SealCoat)
- Must use dewaxed shellac — wax causes fish eyes and poor epoxy wet-out under flood coat
- Must use clear/neutral shellac — colored shellac tints wood and shifts artwork colors
- Seal both faces of glued-up panels to control warping through print + epoxy cycle
- Workflow: sand → seal both sides (dewaxed clear shellac 2-lb cut) → sand back 240 grit → UV print → flood coat clear epoxy
- Failed product: Timgle 35 oz Shellac Flakes (Amazon B0CKP3Z4W1) — waxed and colored, causes fish eyes and tinting
